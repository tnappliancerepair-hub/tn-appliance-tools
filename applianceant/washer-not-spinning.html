Washer · DIY Diagnosis

Washer Not Spinning?Lid, Belt, or Coupler.

A washer that fills and drains but won't spin usually isn't broken deep down. On top-loaders it's most often the lid switch (the machine thinks the lid is open) or, on direct-drive Whirlpool/Kenmore models, a failed motor coupler. On front-loaders it's the door lock or a worn drive belt. An off-balance or overloaded load will also stop the spin — check that first, it's free.

🐜 Straight talk: this is diagnostic help to point you at the likely cause — it's not a guarantee, and every machine is different. Always cut the power (and shut off gas or water) before you touch anything, test to confirm the part is actually bad before you buy, and if it's beyond you, get a pro. No shame in that.

What's usually wrong

The Most Likely Causes

Listed in rough order of how often they turn out to be the culprit — not a diagnosis of your specific machine. Work top-down: the cheap, free checks first, then test before you buy anything.

Off-balance or overloaded load

Modern washers refuse to spin a load that's bunched to one side or packed too full — a safety feature, not a fault. Redistribute and try again before you buy anything.

Bad lid switch (top-load) or door lock (front-load)

If the machine can't confirm the lid/door is shut, it won't spin. A failed switch fakes an open lid. Very common and cheap.

Failed motor coupler (direct-drive top-loaders)

On many Whirlpool/Kenmore/Roper top-loaders a small plastic-and-rubber coupler links the motor to the transmission. It wears out and the tub stops spinning. Cheap part, classic failure.

Worn or broken drive belt

Belt-driven washers (many front-loaders + some top-loaders) won't spin if the belt is stretched, glazed, or snapped. Check if the drum turns freely by hand.

Worn clutch (top-load)

The clutch brings the tub up to spin speed. When it wears, you get a weak spin and wet clothes plus sometimes a burning smell.

Do it the pro way

Test → Estimate → Test

Good techs don't swap parts and hope. They test, price it out, then confirm. Do the same and you won't buy the wrong part — or pour money into a machine that isn't worth it.

1 · Test before you buy

Confirm the suspect part is actually bad — a multimeter continuity check, or a clear visual like a split element or a cracked gasket. Never replace on a guess; that's how the returns pile up.

2 · Estimate honestly

Add up the parts plus your time and compare to a new unit. A cheap part in a well-built machine is a yes. A pricey control board in an old one may not be. Be honest with yourself here.

3 · Test again after

Once it's installed, run a full cycle and confirm the fix before you button everything back up. Two minutes now beats taking it apart twice.

Can you fix it yourself?

Safe to Try · Know the Risk · Call a Pro

General guidance, not professional advice. Always unplug the appliance (or shut off the gas/water) before you check anything. If it feels beyond you, that's exactly when to get a pro.

✅ Safe to try yourself

  • Redistribute the load and make sure it isn't overpacked — the #1 free fix.
  • Confirm the lid fully closes / the front-load door latches.
  • Open the lid and check if the tub spins freely by hand (points to belt or coupler).

⚠️ Doable — know the risk

  • Lid switch, door lock, motor coupler, or belt replacement — very doable, but UNPLUG the washer and unplug the water first. Some need the cabinet opened.

🛑 Call a pro — don't touch this

  • The drive motor or control board.
  • A front-load tub bearing (loud spin) — major teardown.
  • Tipping/opening the machine without help — they're heavy.
Is it worth fixing?

The Honest Call

Usually worth it — lid switches, couplers, and belts are $10–55 and an hour or two. The one to weigh is a front-load tub bearing (a loud, grinding spin): that's a big teardown and on an older machine often tips toward replacing.

FAQ

People Also Ask

Why does my washer fill and drain but not spin?
The motor works but something stops the spin — most often a lid switch/door lock, a worn motor coupler (direct-drive top-loaders), or a broken belt. An off-balance load also stops it; rebalance first.
Why are my clothes still soaking wet after the cycle?
The washer isn't reaching spin speed. Check for an off-balance load, then a bad lid switch, a worn clutch (weak spin), or a slipping belt.
What is a motor coupler and why does it fail?
On direct-drive Whirlpool/Kenmore top-loaders it's a small plastic-and-rubber part linking the motor to the transmission. It's designed to fail first (to protect the motor), so it's a common, cheap fix.
Is it worth fixing a washer that won't spin?
Yes for a lid switch, coupler, belt, or clutch ($10–55). A worn tub bearing on an older front-loader is the pricey exception where replacing can make more sense.
