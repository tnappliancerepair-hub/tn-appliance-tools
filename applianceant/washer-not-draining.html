Washer · DIY Diagnosis

Washer Not Draining?Find the Clog or the Pump.

Standing water almost always means the water can't get through the pump. On front-loaders it's very often the coin-trap filter packed with lint, coins, and socks — a free clean. If the pump hums but won't move water, or is silent, the drain pump itself has failed. Top-loaders add a lid switch that can stop the drain/spin.

🐜 Straight talk: this is diagnostic help to point you at the likely cause — it's not a guarantee, and every machine is different. Always cut the power (and shut off gas or water) before you touch anything, test to confirm the part is actually bad before you buy, and if it's beyond you, get a pro. No shame in that.

What's usually wrong

The Most Likely Causes

Listed in rough order of how often they turn out to be the culprit — not a diagnosis of your specific machine. Work top-down: the cheap, free checks first, then test before you buy anything.

Clogged pump filter / coin trap (front-load)

Front-loaders have a small access door at the bottom front hiding a filter. Lint, coins, and hairpins collect there and block the drain. Cleaning it is free and fixes a huge share of "won't drain" calls.

Failed drain pump

If the filter's clear but the pump hums without moving water — or is dead silent — the drain pump has failed. This is the most common part replacement for this symptom.

Clogged or kinked drain hose

The hose at the back can kink behind the machine or clog where it meets the standpipe. Pull the washer out and check the whole run.

Bad lid switch or door lock (top-load)

Many top-loaders won't spin or drain if the lid switch doesn't register "closed." A failed switch fakes an open lid and stops the drain.

Do it the pro way

Test → Estimate → Test

Good techs don't swap parts and hope. They test, price it out, then confirm. Do the same and you won't buy the wrong part — or pour money into a machine that isn't worth it.

1 · Test before you buy

Confirm the suspect part is actually bad — a multimeter continuity check, or a clear visual like a split element or a cracked gasket. Never replace on a guess; that's how the returns pile up.

2 · Estimate honestly

Add up the parts plus your time and compare to a new unit. A cheap part in a well-built machine is a yes. A pricey control board in an old one may not be. Be honest with yourself here.

3 · Test again after

Once it's installed, run a full cycle and confirm the fix before you button everything back up. Two minutes now beats taking it apart twice.

Can you fix it yourself?

Safe to Try · Know the Risk · Call a Pro

General guidance, not professional advice. Always unplug the appliance (or shut off the gas/water) before you check anything. If it feels beyond you, that's exactly when to get a pro.

✅ Safe to try yourself

  • Clean the front-load pump filter (coin trap) — towel down first.
  • Pull the machine out and check the drain hose for kinks or clogs at the standpipe.
  • Run a drain/spin-only cycle to confirm the symptom before buying anything.

⚠️ Doable — know the risk

  • Drain pump or lid-switch replacement — very doable, but UNPLUG the washer and turn off the water first. Expect leftover water in the tub.

🛑 Call a pro — don't touch this

  • Any repair that means tipping the machine and you don't have help — front-loaders are heavy.
  • A leak you can't trace, or water reaching the motor/control board.
Is it worth fixing?

The Honest Call

Usually worth it. A pump filter clean is free; a drain pump is $25–70 and about an hour. A new washer is $600+. Unless the drum bearing is also gone (a loud repair), draining issues are among the most cost-effective DIY fixes there are.

FAQ

People Also Ask

Why is my washer full of water and not draining?
The water can't get past the pump. Check the front-load pump filter first (free), then the drain hose, then the drain pump itself. Top-loaders can also stop draining on a bad lid switch.
Where is the drain pump filter on a front-load washer?
Behind a small access panel at the bottom front. Twist the cap counter-clockwise to open — keep a towel and shallow pan ready, water will come out.
Is a front-load drain pump the same as a top-load one?
No. They're different parts even within the same brand. Always buy the pump listed for your exact model number.
How do I know if my drain pump is bad?
If the filter and hose are clear but the pump buzzes without moving water, or makes no sound at all during the drain cycle, the pump has failed.
