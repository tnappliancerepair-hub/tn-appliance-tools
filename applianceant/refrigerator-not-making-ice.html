Refrigerator · DIY Diagnosis

Ice Maker Not Working?Filter, Valve, or Module.

Work cheapest-first. An overdue water filter or a frozen fill tube stops ice and costs little or nothing to fix. If water isn't reaching the tray, the water inlet valve is the usual part. If the tray fills but never ejects, the ice maker module/motor has failed. And check the obvious: the ice maker's on/off arm or switch may just be OFF.

🐜 Straight talk: this is diagnostic help to point you at the likely cause — it's not a guarantee, and every machine is different. Always cut the power (and shut off gas or water) before you touch anything, test to confirm the part is actually bad before you buy, and if it's beyond you, get a pro. No shame in that.

What's usually wrong

The Most Likely Causes

Listed in rough order of how often they turn out to be the culprit — not a diagnosis of your specific machine. Work top-down: the cheap, free checks first, then test before you buy anything.

Ice maker turned off, or a full-bin arm stuck

The metal feeler arm (or a switch) shuts the ice maker off when the bin is full — it can get bumped to OFF or stuck up. Free to check first.

Clogged or overdue water filter

A filter past its 6-month life chokes water flow to the ice maker (and the dispenser). The single most common ice fix.

Frozen fill tube / water line

The small tube that fills the ice tray can freeze shut, especially if the freezer's too cold. Thaw it (a hair dryer on low) — free.

Failed water inlet valve

This valve opens to send water to the ice maker. When it fails, no water reaches the tray. Needs adequate house water pressure (40+ psi) to work.

Failed ice maker module/motor

If water fills the tray but it never dumps the cubes, the ice maker assembly (the motorized module) has failed. It's a bolt-in replacement.

Do it the pro way

Test → Estimate → Test

Good techs don't swap parts and hope. They test, price it out, then confirm. Do the same and you won't buy the wrong part — or pour money into a machine that isn't worth it.

1 · Test before you buy

Confirm the suspect part is actually bad — a multimeter continuity check, or a clear visual like a split element or a cracked gasket. Never replace on a guess; that's how the returns pile up.

2 · Estimate honestly

Add up the parts plus your time and compare to a new unit. A cheap part in a well-built machine is a yes. A pricey control board in an old one may not be. Be honest with yourself here.

3 · Test again after

Once it's installed, run a full cycle and confirm the fix before you button everything back up. Two minutes now beats taking it apart twice.

Can you fix it yourself?

Safe to Try · Know the Risk · Call a Pro

General guidance, not professional advice. Always unplug the appliance (or shut off the gas/water) before you check anything. If it feels beyond you, that's exactly when to get a pro.

✅ Safe to try yourself

  • Confirm the ice maker is switched ON and the arm is down.
  • Replace the water filter if it's older than 6 months.
  • Thaw a frozen fill tube with a hair dryer on low; check the water line isn't kinked.

⚠️ Doable — know the risk

  • Water inlet valve or ice maker assembly replacement — doable, but UNPLUG the fridge and shut off the water supply first.

🛑 Call a pro — don't touch this

  • A sealed-system frost problem behind the ice maker.
  • Running a new water line into the wall/plumbing.
Is it worth fixing?

The Honest Call

Yes. The filter, valve, and module are $25–120 — a fraction of a new fridge, and no-ice never justifies replacing an otherwise-good refrigerator. Do the free checks (switch, filter, frozen tube) before buying anything.

FAQ

People Also Ask

Why did my refrigerator ice maker suddenly stop?
Most often an overdue water filter, a frozen fill tube, or a failed water inlet valve. First confirm the ice maker is switched on and give it 24 hours after any fix.
How often should I change the refrigerator water filter?
About every 6 months. An old filter chokes flow to both the ice maker and the water dispenser — it's the most common ice-maker culprit.
Why does water fill the tray but no ice comes out?
The ice maker module/motor that ejects the cubes has failed. It's usually a bolt-in replacement matched to your model.
Do I need good water pressure for an ice maker?
Yes — most need roughly 40+ psi. Low house pressure or a partly-closed saddle valve can starve the ice maker even with a good filter and valve.
