My oven temperature is off — it runs hot or cold, or food won't cook right. What do I do?

When your oven bakes too dark, too pale, or takes forever, the temperature it's actually holding doesn't match what you set. Sometimes that's a simple calibration; sometimes a part is drifting. Here's how a tech figures out which — starting with a quick test you can do yourself.

⚠ Safety first: Low-risk to test with a thermometer. Oven elements and sensors run on 240V (or gas) — any part replacement is done by a tech with the power off.

The most likely causes

It just needs calibration Easy DIY check

Ovens can be off by 25–50°F and still be "working" — and most have a built-in calibration adjustment. A new oven, or one that's always run a little off, often just needs its offset set.

Check it: Put an oven thermometer in the center, set 350°F, let it fully preheat and cycle, and read it. If it's off by a consistent amount, your oven's settings menu usually has a temperature-offset adjustment (check the manual).

Drifted oven temperature sensor Moderate DIY check

The sensor that tells the control the oven temp can drift out of spec with age, so the oven holds the wrong temperature — often running cold or unevenly.

Check it: If calibration doesn't bring it in line, a tech ohms the sensor against spec and replaces it if it's off — an affordable part.

Weak bake element or igniter Moderate DIY check

On electric, a bake element that's partially failed heats weakly (pale tops, long preheat). On gas, a weak igniter makes the burner cycle slowly, so the oven can't hold temp.

Check it: On electric, watch if the bake element glows fully and evenly. On gas, watch if the igniter glows a long time before the flame lights. Either weak part is a tech replacement.

Convection fan or control issue Technician

On convection ovens a failing fan cooks unevenly; a control fault can also misfire the heat.

Check it: If temperature is erratic rather than just offset, a tech checks the convection fan and control.

Is it worth fixing?

Repair — a calibration is free, and a sensor, element, or igniter is an affordable part plus flat labor. An oven that's just off temperature is well worth fixing.

Common questions

How do I know if my oven temperature is accurate?

Put an oven thermometer in the center, set 350°F, let it fully preheat and cycle a few times, and read it. If it's consistently off, you can usually adjust the oven's temperature offset in its settings — check your manual.

Why does my oven run cold / not get hot enough?

Usually a drifted temperature sensor or a weak bake element (electric) or igniter (gas). Try calibrating first; if it's still off, those parts are an affordable tech fix.
