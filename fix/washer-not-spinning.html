My washer won't spin — what do I do?

Clothes come out sopping wet because the spin never happened. On most machines this traces to one of a few parts — the lid switch/door lock, the drive system (belt or coupler), or a drain problem that stops the spin from ever starting. Below are the causes in the order we check them, the actual tests (with the multimeter readings), the parts and honest prices, and the safe checks you can do first.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the washer before testing switches or touching the drive system. If it also didn't drain, there's trapped water — keep towels ready. Test electrical parts only with the machine unplugged.

How a washer's spin works (30 seconds)

Before it can spin, the control needs two green lights: the lid switch / door lock must say the door is secure, and the tub must be drained. Then the motor drives the tub — through a drive belt (belt-drive machines) or a motor coupler / direct-drive transmission. A lid lock that won't engage, a machine that won't drain, a worn belt, or a broken coupler all kill the spin. Front-loaders add a door lock and shock absorbers — a badly off-balance load can make the control abort the spin.
What we see most: on top-loaders it's the lid switch/lock and — on older Whirlpool/Kenmore direct-drive machines — the motor coupler. On front-loaders, a door lock or a drain fault (it won't spin until it drains) leads. Always check first: did it actually drain? No drain = no spin.

The most likely causes — in the order we check them

It didn't drain first Easy DIY check

Most washers won't advance to spin until the water is gone. A drain problem shows up as "won't spin" even though the spin system is fine.

Test it yourself: Is there standing water? If so, fix the drain first — clear the pump filter/coin trap and check the drain hose. Once it drains, the spin usually returns on its own.

🔩 Likely part: usually just cleaning — see the won't-drain guide

Tools: Pan + towelsTime: 10–20 min

Lid switch / door lock Moderate DIY

The #1 no-spin part. If the control doesn't see the lid/door as securely closed, it won't drain or spin — the machine acts "stuck" even though the motor is fine.

Test it yourself: Unplug the washer and put a multimeter on the switch/lock terminals — it should read continuity (near 0 ohms) when latched and open when released. No change when you latch it = failed switch. On top-loaders, listen for the click when you press the lid down.

🔩 Likely part: lid switch / door lock — $20–60

Tools: Multimeter, screwdriversTime: 30–60 min

Worn or broken drive belt Moderate DIY

On belt-drive washers, a stretched or broken belt slips — the motor runs but the tub barely turns or doesn't spin.

Test it yourself: Unplug and access the belt (usually behind a rear or bottom panel). Look for cracks, glazing, or looseness. If the tub spins freely by hand but the belt is loose/cracked, that's it.

🔩 Likely part: drive belt — $10–30

Tools: Nut driver / socket setTime: 45–90 min

Motor coupler (direct-drive) Technician

Older Whirlpool/Kenmore direct-drive washers use a plastic-and-rubber coupler between the motor and transmission. It wears out — the motor runs but there's no agitation or spin.

How a tech tests it: If the motor hums/runs but you get no agitation and no spin, and drain + lid switch are good, the coupler is the classic culprit — inspection confirms the worn/broken coupler.

🔩 Likely part: motor coupler — $10–25

Tools: Nut driver, putty knifeTime: 1–1.5 hrs (tech)

Drive motor or control board Technician

Less common: the drive motor fails, or the control board never sends the spin command.

How a tech tests it: With power off, the motor windings are tested for continuity — an open reading = bad motor. If drain, lid switch, belt, and coupler all check out, the board is tested next (it's the pricier part, so we confirm before replacing).

🔩 Likely part: drive motor $80–200 · control board varies

Tools: MultimeterTime: 1–2 hrs (tech)

Off-balance load or worn tub bearings (front-load) Technician

Front-loaders abort the spin when the load is badly off-balance; a very loud spin can mean worn tub bearings.

Test it yourself: Redistribute the load evenly and try again — a single heavy item (a rug, a hoodie) often trips the balance sensor. A loud, grinding spin that still works points to bearings (a technician repair).

🔩 Likely part: none (rebalance) — or tub bearings (tech job)

Tools: None for the rebalanceTime: 5 min

Tools you'll need for the DIY checks

  • Multimeter (lid switch / motor tests)
  • Nut driver or socket set (belt + panels)
  • Putty knife (to release top-load cabinet clips)
  • Flashlight, pan + towels

Repair or replace? (the honest math)

A lid switch, door lock, drive belt, or motor coupler is a cheap fix — parts are typically $10–60 — and worth doing on almost any washer. It only tips toward replacement when a no-spin is paired with a failed drive motor and worn tub bearings, or a bad board on an older machine. Common questions

Why won't my washer spin?

Spin won't start unless the lid switch/door lock says the door is secure and the water has drained. Most common: a failed lid switch/door lock, a machine that won't drain, a worn drive belt, or (older direct-drive) a broken motor coupler.

Why are my clothes still soaking wet?

The spin never happened or it didn't drain. Check first whether it actually drained — standing water means fix the drain and the spin usually returns. If it drained but didn't spin, suspect the lid switch, belt, or coupler.

How do I test a lid switch or door lock?

Unplug the washer and put a multimeter on the switch/lock terminals — it should read continuity (near 0 ohms) when latched and open when released. No change means it's failed.

Can I fix a washer that won't spin myself?

Often yes — rebalancing the load, clearing a drain, testing the lid switch, and swapping a drive belt are DIY-friendly with the machine unplugged. A drive motor or tub bearings are technician repairs.

Is a washer that won't spin worth fixing?

Usually — lid switch, belt, and coupler are cheap. Only a failed motor or worn bearings get pricey, and we give honest repair-vs-replace numbers for those. Send a short video and we'll tell you the exact part.
