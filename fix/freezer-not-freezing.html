My freezer isn't freezing — what do I do?

A freezer that hums along but won't get cold is racing a clock — your food. On chest and upright freezers the cause is almost always one of a short list: the defrost system, the fan that moves cold air, the little relay that starts the compressor, or frost that has quietly blocked the airflow. Here are the real causes in the order a technician checks them, plus the safe things you can look at yourself first.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the freezer before checking the relay or fan, and don't attempt any sealed-system or refrigerant work yourself — that's licensed EPA-608 work.

The most likely causes

Failed defrost system (heater, thermostat, or timer) Moderate DIY check

On frost-free freezers a defrost cycle melts frost off the coils periodically. When the heater, defrost thermostat, or timer/control fails, frost builds on the evaporator until it blocks airflow and the freezer stops cooling — even though the compressor runs.

Check it: Unplug the freezer and look at the evaporator coils (behind the back inner panel). Heavy solid ice there points straight at a defrost failure. Let it fully thaw 24 hours, plug back in — if it cools then slowly frosts up again, the defrost system needs a part.

Stuck or failed evaporator fan Moderate DIY check

The evaporator fan pulls cold air across the coils and through the freezer. If it's iced up or the motor has failed, the coils get cold but the cold never circulates.

Check it: With the freezer unplugged, check whether the fan blade spins freely by hand and isn't jammed by ice. A grinding or silent fan on a freezer that should be running is a strong clue.

Bad compressor start relay Technician

The start relay kicks the compressor on. When it fails, the compressor either won't start or clicks on and off every few minutes — very common on chest and deep freezers. You'll often hear a click every 2–5 minutes with no real cooling.

Check it: Listen for repeated clicking with no steady compressor hum. This is a diagnosis clue only — the relay swap should be done by a tech to confirm the compressor itself is healthy.

Dirty condenser coils / blocked airflow Easy DIY check

Dust-caked condenser coils or a freezer jammed against a wall can't shed heat, so it runs constantly and struggles to hold temperature — worse in a hot garage.

Check it: Unplug it, vacuum the condenser coils (back or bottom), and give it a few inches of breathing room on all sides.

Sealed-system leak or dead compressor Technician

If the coils never get cold and the compressor runs warm, the sealed system may have lost refrigerant or the compressor has failed. This is licensed refrigerant work.

Check it: Nothing safe to DIY here — recovery, leak repair, and recharge require EPA-608 certification and the right tools.

Is it worth fixing?

Most freezer failures — defrost parts, relays, thermostats, fans — are inexpensive and well worth fixing, especially since replacing a freezer usually means losing the food inside while you shop. Common questions

Why is my freezer running but not freezing?

The compressor running while nothing freezes points to a defrost failure (frost blocking the coils), a stuck evaporator fan, or a sealed-system problem. Check the coils behind the back panel for heavy ice first — that's the most common cause.

Why does my chest freezer click but not get cold?

A click every few minutes with no cooling is the classic sign of a failed compressor start relay. It's an affordable, common repair — but the relay should be swapped by a tech to confirm the compressor itself is still good.

Is a freezer worth repairing?

Usually yes. Freezers are simple, durable machines and most repairs cost a fraction of replacement — plus you keep your food. We give you honest repair-vs-replace numbers on your exact model.
