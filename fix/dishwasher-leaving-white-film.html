My dishwasher leaves a white film on dishes — what do I do?

A white, cloudy film on dishes after a wash is one of the most common dishwasher complaints, and the good news is it's almost always a settings-or-water issue you can fix yourself, not a broken machine. The one thing to rule out is permanent glass etching. Here's how a tech sorts it out, cheapest first.

⚠ Safety first: All low-risk cleaning and settings steps — no tools or power work needed.

The most likely causes

Hard water mineral deposits (the #1 cause) Easy DIY check

Hard water leaves behind calcium and magnesium as a chalky white film on glasses and the tub, especially without enough rinse aid.

Check it: Run an empty hot cycle with a cup of white vinegar in a bowl on the top rack (or a dishwasher cleaner). For ongoing hard water, use a rinse aid and consider a dishwasher water-softening additive. This clears most white film.

No (or empty) rinse aid Easy DIY check

Rinse aid helps water sheet off dishes so minerals don't dry on as spots and film. An empty dispenser is a top cause of cloudy glasses.

Check it: Fill the rinse-aid dispenser and set it to a higher level. On many machines this fixes filming and spotting immediately.

Too much detergent, or the wrong kind Easy DIY check

Excess detergent (or a powder that doesn't dissolve in hard/cold water) leaves a residue film. Old or clumped detergent is worse.

Check it: Use the recommended amount of a fresh detergent (pods meter it for you). Make sure your hot water reaches the dishwasher — run the kitchen tap hot before starting.

Clogged filter or spray arms Easy DIY check

A dirty filter or blocked spray-arm holes means dishes aren't rinsed well, so residue and film are left behind.

Check it: Clean the bottom filter in hot soapy water and clear the spray-arm holes with a toothpick. Do the filter monthly.

Permanent glass etching (not fixable) Easy DIY check

If the film won't come off even with vinegar, the glass may be permanently etched — micro-pitting from soft water plus too much detergent over time. This is damage, not residue.

Check it: Test one cloudy glass with a vinegar soak: if it clears, it was mineral film (fixable with the steps above); if it stays cloudy, it's etched and permanent. Going forward, cut detergent and use rinse aid to prevent more.

Is it worth fixing?

Almost always a free fix — rinse aid, the right detergent amount, a vinegar cycle, and a clean filter solve the vast majority of white film. There's rarely a part to replace. Only permanent etching can't be reversed, and that's a habit change, not a repair.

Common questions

Why does my dishwasher leave a white film on dishes?

Almost always hard-water minerals, an empty rinse-aid dispenser, or too much detergent. Fill the rinse aid, use the right amount of fresh detergent, and run an empty hot cycle with a cup of white vinegar to strip existing buildup. Clean the filter too. That clears nearly all white film.

How do I get white residue off my dishes and dishwasher?

Run an empty cycle with a cup of white vinegar in a bowl on the top rack (or a dishwasher cleaner) to dissolve mineral buildup, and start using rinse aid. For the dishes, a vinegar soak clears mineral film — if it doesn't, the glass is etched (permanent).

Is white film on dishes permanent?

Usually no — mineral film and detergent residue wipe or vinegar off. But if a cloudy glass stays cloudy after a vinegar soak, it's etched, which is permanent micro-damage from soft water plus too much detergent. Cutting detergent and using rinse aid prevents more.
