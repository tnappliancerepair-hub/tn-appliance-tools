What does the F2E1 error code mean on my Whirlpool oven or range?

F2E1 on a Whirlpool oven means the control detected a stuck key — the touchpad is telling the control a button is being pressed when it isn't, so the oven stops responding normally and often beeps. It's a control-panel problem, not a heating problem, and it's a well-defined fault. Here's how a tech works it.

⚠ Safety first: Low-risk to try a reset. Any control/touchpad replacement is done by a tech with the oven powered off at the breaker.

The most likely causes

Temporary glitch (try a reset first) Easy DIY check

Sometimes a brief control glitch throws F2E1 once. A power reset clears a one-time occurrence.

Check it: Turn the oven off at the breaker for a full minute, then back on. If F2E1 doesn't come back, it was a one-time glitch.

Failed touchpad / membrane Technician

The most common real cause. The membrane behind the touch panel wears or shorts, so it reports a key press that isn't happening — a genuine stuck key.

Check it: A tech can disconnect the keypad ribbon from the control; if the fault clears with it unplugged, the touchpad/membrane is bad and gets replaced.

Ribbon connector or control board Technician

A poorly-seated or corroded ribbon connector between the touchpad and control, or a fault on the control board itself, can also produce F2E1.

Check it: A tech reseats/inspects the ribbon and, if the touchpad tests good, looks at the control board.

Is it worth fixing?

Repair — a touchpad or control is an affordable part plus flat labor, well below a new range. Start with the free reset; if it returns, it's a straightforward touchpad/control fix.

Common questions

How do I fix the F2E1 code on my Whirlpool oven?

First cut power at the breaker for a minute to clear a possible glitch. If F2E1 comes back, the touchpad/membrane is reporting a stuck key — a tech disconnects the keypad ribbon to confirm and replaces the touchpad or control.

What does "stuck key" mean on a Whirlpool oven?

It means the control thinks a button is being held down when it isn't, because the touch membrane has shorted or worn. The oven can't operate normally until the touchpad (or its connection) is fixed.
