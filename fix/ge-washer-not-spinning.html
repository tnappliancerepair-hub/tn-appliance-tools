GE Washer Won'T Spin?

GE top-loaders most often won't spin because of the lid switch, the drain, or — on older belt-less GE models — the motor coupler or mode shifter. GE won't spin with the lid up or water still in the tub. The lid switch is one of the most common, cheapest fixes.
The known GE issue: The GE top-load lid switch is one of the cheapest, most common appliance fixes there is — if a GE fills and agitates but won't spin, start there before anything else.

What's actually different about a GE

Lid switch failed (top-load) Moderate

GE top-loaders won't spin if the lid switch doesn't confirm the lid is down. A worn lid switch is the #1 "won't spin" cause and a very cheap part.
What you can check: If the washer fills and agitates but won't spin, the lid switch is the prime suspect — an inexpensive, common fix.

Won't drain (clogged pump) Moderate

No drain, no spin. A clogged pump or hose leaves water in the tub so the spin never starts.
What you can check: Check the drain pump and hose for a clog and clear it.

Mode shifter / motor coupler Pro

On many GE top-loaders a failed mode shifter (or worn motor coupler on older models) stops the spin while other functions still work.
What you can check: If the lid switch and drain are good, a tech checks the mode shifter/coupler — common, affordable parts.

Door lock or belt (front-load) Pro

A front-load GE won't spin with a failed door lock, and a worn belt lets the motor run without turning the drum.
What you can check: Confirm the door locks; no drum motion with a running motor points to the belt.

How this failure works on any washer

A washer that won't spin leaves you with a soaking, heavy load. The first thing to know: a machine won't spin until it has fully drained — so a spin problem is very often really a drain problem. After that, it's usually a switch, a belt, or (on top-loaders) the coupler. Here's how we sort it out.

Safety first: Unplug the washer before checking the lid switch, belt, or coupler, and redistribute a wet load carefully — it's heavy.

It hasn't fully drained Easy

Most washers refuse to spin until the water is out. If there's standing water, fix the drain issue first — the spin will usually return with it.
What you can check: Check for standing water and clear the pump filter/drain hose (see our washer-won't-drain guide).

Unbalanced load Easy

A bulky or lopsided load (a comforter, towels bunched to one side) trips the balance sensor and cancels the spin to protect the machine.
What you can check: Open the lid, redistribute the load evenly, and restart the spin.

Lid switch or door lock Pro

The machine won't spin if it doesn't sense a securely closed lid or locked door. A failed switch is a common cause.
What you can check: Confirm the lid latches firmly and you hear/feel it engage. A failed switch is a technician repair.

Motor coupler (top-loaders) Pro

On many top-load washers a plastic coupler connects the motor to the transmission and wears out — the washer runs but won't agitate or spin.
What you can check: Not a DIY check, but a very common and affordable repair on top-loaders.

Drive belt Pro

A stretched, slipped, or broken belt stops the drum from spinning.
What you can check: Not a DIY check. A technician inspects and replaces the belt.

Is it worth repairing?

Lid switches, couplers, and belts are all inexpensive parts with reasonable labor — a no-spin washer is almost always worth repairing. The only time to reconsider is when the no-spin is paired with bad main bearings (a loud, expensive repair) on an older machine.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my GE washer won't spin?

GE top-loaders most often won't spin because of the lid switch, the drain, or — on older belt-less GE models — the motor coupler or mode shifter. GE won't spin with the lid up or water still in the tub. The lid switch is one of the most common, cheapest fixes.

Is there a known issue with GE washers won't spin?

The GE top-load lid switch is one of the cheapest, most common appliance fixes there is — if a GE fills and agitates but won't spin, start there before anything else.

Why won't my washer spin the water out?

Most washers won't spin until they've fully drained, so a no-spin problem is often really a drain problem. Beyond that, the common causes are an unbalanced load, a failed lid switch or door lock, or a worn belt or motor coupler.

My washer fills and washes but won't spin — what's wrong?

If it agitates but won't spin, look at the drain first (it must be empty to spin), then the lid switch, and on top-loaders the motor coupler. On front-loaders, the door lock and belt are common causes.

Is a washer that won't spin worth fixing?

Usually yes — lid switches, couplers, and belts are affordable. Reconsider only if it's paired with worn bearings on an older machine, and we'll give you the honest numbers.
