My washer smells like mildew or mold — what do I do?

Answered by Teddy Pivacek, owner & lead technician at TN Appliance Exchange — repairing appliances since 2012.

Quick answer: Moisture plus detergent residue feeds mold and bacteria in the spots that stay wet — most often the front-load door gasket, the detergent drawer, and the drain filter. Cleaning those areas and running a hot tub-clean cycle clears the smell.

That musty, mildew smell — especially in a front-loader — comes from moisture and detergent residue feeding mold and bacteria in the spots that never fully dry. It's extremely common and, in most cases, something you can clean and prevent yourself. Here's where the smell hides and how we get rid of it.

⚠ Safety first: Use gloves when cleaning the gasket, keep the room ventilated, and never mix bleach with other cleaners.

The most likely causes

Biofilm in the door gasket (front-loaders) Easy DIY check

The rubber door boot has folds that trap water, lint, and detergent — the perfect home for black, musty mold. This is the #1 source of the smell in front-load washers.

Check it: Pull back the folds of the rubber gasket and wipe with a 1:1 water-vinegar or diluted-bleach solution. Get into every fold — that's where it lives. Dry it after.

Detergent and softener buildup Easy DIY check

Using too much detergent, or liquid fabric softener, leaves a sticky residue in the drum, dispenser, and hoses that grows bacteria and smells.

Check it: Run a hot tub-clean cycle (or hottest cycle) with a washer-cleaning tablet or plain white vinegar, empty. Switch to the correct amount of HE detergent going forward.

Standing water in the drain filter or hose Moderate DIY check

Trapped water in the pump filter (coin trap) or a sagging drain hose sits and turns sour.

Check it: On front-loaders, open the small filter panel at the bottom front (towels ready), drain and clean it. Make sure the drain hose isn't pushed too deep or kinked.

Leaving wet laundry or the door closed Easy DIY check

Wet clothes left in the drum, or sealing a damp washer shut after every load, keeps moisture in so mold thrives.

Check it: Move loads to the dryer promptly, and leave the door (and dispenser drawer) cracked open between washes so the drum dries out.

Grime in the dispenser drawer Easy DIY check

The detergent/softener drawer and its housing collect gunk that molds.

Check it: Remove the dispenser drawer (most pull out with a tab), scrub it and the cavity, and rinse before replacing.

Is it worth fixing?

A mildew smell is almost always a cleaning-and-habit fix, not a repair — no parts needed in most cases. If the odor persists after a thorough clean, a worn gasket or a drain issue may need a technician, but that's uncommon and still affordable.

Common questions

Why does my washing machine smell like mildew?

Moisture plus detergent residue feeds mold and bacteria in the spots that stay wet — most often the front-load door gasket, the detergent drawer, and the drain filter. Cleaning those areas and running a hot tub-clean cycle clears the smell.

How do I get the mildew smell out of my front-load washer?

Wipe out every fold of the rubber door gasket with a vinegar or diluted-bleach solution, clean the dispenser drawer, drain and clean the pump filter, then run an empty hot tub-clean cycle. Afterward, leave the door cracked open between loads to keep it dry.

Is a smelly washer something I need a technician for?

Usually not — it's a cleaning and habit fix (right amount of HE detergent, door left ajar, regular tub-clean cycles). Only if the smell persists after a deep clean would a worn gasket or drain problem warrant a technician, and those are affordable repairs.
