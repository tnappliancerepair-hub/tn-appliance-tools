One of my electric stove burners won't heat — what do I do?

When one electric burner won't heat while the others work, the good news is the problem is isolated to that burner's parts — the element, its connection, or its switch. Here's how a tech narrows it down, starting with a swap test you can do yourself on coil-style ranges.

⚠ Safety first: ⚠️ Electric ranges run on 240V. Always turn the burner and range OFF (and let it cool) before touching an element. Any switch or wiring work behind the control is done by a tech with power off at the breaker.

The most likely causes

Bad heating element (coil) Easy DIY check

On coil-top ranges, the element itself burns out — often with a visible blister, break, or scorch. It's the most common single-burner failure.

Check it: On a coil range: with everything cool, swap the dead coil with a same-size working one. If the problem follows the coil to the new spot, the coil is bad (a cheap, plug-in replacement). If the spot stays dead, it's the socket/switch.

Worn or burned coil socket (receptacle) Moderate DIY check

The socket the coil plugs into can burn or lose tension, so the element doesn't get good contact and won't heat.

Check it: If the swap test shows the SPOT is dead (a known-good coil won't heat there), the socket/receptacle is likely burned — an affordable tech replacement.

Failed burner switch Technician

The infinite switch behind the knob controls power to that burner. When it fails, the burner won't heat or won't regulate.

Check it: A dead spot with a good coil and socket points at the switch — a tech tests and replaces it.

Smooth-top element or control Technician

On glass/smooth-top ranges you can't swap elements — a dead radiant element or its control needs testing.

Check it: On a smooth top, a single dead element is a tech diagnosis (element or control board).

Is it worth fixing?

Repair — a coil element is cheap (often a quick DIY on coil ranges), and a socket or switch is an affordable tech part. A single dead burner almost never justifies replacing the range.

Common questions

Why is one burner on my electric stove not working?

The problem is isolated to that burner — usually the element (coil), its socket, or its switch. On a coil range, swap the dead coil with a good one: if the fault follows the coil, replace the coil; if the spot stays dead, it's the socket or switch.

How do I test an electric stove burner?

On a coil range, with everything cool, swap the non-working coil with a same-size working one. Fault follows the coil = bad coil (cheap replacement). Spot stays dead = burned socket or failed switch, which a tech replaces.
