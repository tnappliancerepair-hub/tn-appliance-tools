LG Refrigerator Not Cooling?

When an LG refrigerator stops cooling and BOTH the fridge and freezer are warm, the number-one suspect is the linear (inverter) compressor. LG's linear compressors from roughly 2014–2019 had a well-documented failure rate that led to a class-action settlement — and the good news is they carry a 10-year compressor warranty, so the part is often covered.
The known LG issue: LG linear-compressor fridges (2014–2019) are the single most-searched "not cooling" complaint in the country. Before paying for a compressor, verify warranty coverage — LG covers the linear compressor for 10 years and settled a class action over these failures.
🔌 How to test it yourself — power → switch → load

Almost every fault is one broken link in a simple circuit, found with two multimeter measurements — voltage (is power getting here?) and continuity (is the path/part unbroken?).

  1. Power: confirm the outlet/cord delivers the expected voltage (120V, or 240V for dryers/ranges). No power here = it is the outlet or breaker, not the appliance.
  2. Switch/control: unplug, then test switches, thermostats, and fuses for continuity — ~0Ω closed, OL = open/failed.
  3. Load: unplug, then test the element/motor/pump/valve for continuity — a good load reads its resistance; OL = replace it.

Cheat-sheet: ~0Ω/beep = good path · OL/infinite = broken/dead part · 120V/240V present = power is arriving. Full method + every multimeter symbol explained → how to diagnose any appliance.

What's actually different about a LG

Linear/inverter compressor failure Pro

Both compartments warm, compressor not running (no gentle hum) or clicking on and off = the linear compressor or its inverter control board. This is THE common LG failure.
What you can check: Check your model/serial against the LG settlement and warranty — the compressor is often covered 10 years. Diagnosis and replacement are a technician job.

Dirty condenser coils / condenser fan Easy

Coils packed with dust make the compressor overheat and cut out.
What you can check: Unplug, pull the fridge out, vacuum the condenser coils at the bottom/back. Free and safe.

Evaporator fan or defrost Pro

If only the fridge is warm and the freezer is fine, it's more likely the evap fan or defrost than the compressor.
What you can check: Listen for the evap fan behind the freezer panel.

How this failure works on any refrigerator

A refrigerator that's running but not getting cold is stressful — you're watching food go warm. Before you assume the worst (an expensive compressor), know that most "not cooling" calls we get turn out to be an affordable fix: airflow, a fan, or the defrost system. Here's how we work through it, and what you can safely check right now.

Safety first: Unplug the refrigerator before cleaning coils or checking fans. Sealed-system and compressor work requires a licensed technician — never attempt it yourself.

Dirty condenser coils Easy

Coils caked in dust and pet hair can't release heat, so the fridge runs constantly and still won't cool. This is the most common and most preventable cause.
What you can check: Unplug the fridge, find the coils (behind the kick plate or on the back), and vacuum them clean.

Evaporator fan motor Pro

This fan moves cold air from the freezer coils into the fridge compartment. If it fails, the freezer may stay cold-ish while the fridge section warms up.
What you can check: Listen: open the freezer and press the door switch — you should hear the fan. Silence points here. Replacement is a technician repair.

Defrost system failure Pro

Frost builds on the evaporator coils and blocks airflow when the defrost heater, thermostat, or timer/board fails. Classic sign: freezer works, fridge doesn't, and you see frost buildup.
What you can check: If you see heavy frost on the back freezer wall, that's the clue. The repair is a technician job.

Condenser fan motor Pro

On coil-in-back-cabinet models, this fan cools the compressor and coils. If it stops, the system overheats and cooling drops.
What you can check: Not a safe DIY check. Diagnosed by a technician.

Start relay / compressor Pro

The compressor is the heart of the system. A failed start relay (cheap) can stop it from running; a failed compressor (expensive) is the rare worst case.
What you can check: Not DIY. A technician confirms whether it's the affordable relay or the compressor itself.

Overpacked or blocked vents Easy

Blocking the interior air vents with food stops cold air from circulating.
What you can check: Make sure nothing is jammed against the vents inside the fridge and freezer.

Coils, fans, start relays, and defrost parts are all affordable and worth fixing.
