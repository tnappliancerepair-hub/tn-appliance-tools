My dryer runs but won't heat — what do I do?

The drum turns but your clothes come out wet — this almost always traces to a few inexpensive causes, usually tied to airflow or one heating part. Below are the causes in the order we check them, the actual tests (with readings), the parts and honest prices, and the safe checks you can do first. The #1 thing: clean the vent — it's about drying AND fire safety.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the dryer (electric: switch off the double-pole breaker) before any internal check. Gas dryer + any gas smell → stop, ventilate, call a pro. A clogged dryer vent is the leading cause of dryer fires — keep it clean.

How a dryer heats (30 seconds)

Air is pulled in, heated by an electric element or a gas burner (igniter + valve), blown through the drum, and pushed out the vent. Safety devices — a thermal fuse and thermostats — cut the heat if it ever overheats. A blocked vent is the usual root cause: it overheats, trips a safety device, and now the drum turns with no heat. Electric dryers also run on 240V, so losing half the circuit leaves the drum spinning but cold.
What we see most: on electric dryers, a blocked vent that blew the thermal fuse is the classic combo — and if you replace the fuse without clearing the vent, the new one blows again. On gas dryers, a weak igniter is the most common no-heat by far.

The most likely causes — in the order we check them

Blocked vent / lint buildup Easy DIY check

Poor airflow makes the dryer overheat and trip its safety devices; then it tumbles with no heat. Most common — and most dangerous.

Test it yourself: Clean the lint screen and the entire vent duct to the outside. Run the dryer and check that the exterior wall flap opens with strong airflow. Weak or no airflow outside = still blocked.

🔩 Likely part: no part — cleaning (or a vent duct if crushed) — $0–20

Tools: Vent brush, VacuumTime: 20–40 min

Blown thermal fuse Moderate DIY

A one-time safety fuse that blows from overheating (usually caused by a blocked vent). Once blown, the drum turns but there's no heat.

Test it yourself: With the dryer unplugged, put a multimeter on the thermal fuse: a good fuse reads continuity (near 0 ohms). Open (OL) = blown — replace it AND clear the vent, or the new one blows too.

🔩 Likely part: thermal fuse — $5–15

Tools: Multimeter, Nut driverTime: 30–60 min

Heating element (electric) / igniter (gas) Technician

An electric element burns out, or a gas igniter weakens and can't open the gas valve — so you get air but no heat.

How a tech tests it: Electric: multimeter across the element reads roughly 10–50 ohms; open = burned out (you may also see a visible break). Gas: watch the igniter — it glows, then should ignite the burner; glows-but-no-flame usually means a weak igniter (a tech confirms with an amp/flame-sensor check).

🔩 Likely part: heating element (electric) or igniter (gas) — $25–60

Tools: Multimeter, Nut driverTime: 1–1.5 hrs (tech)

Half-power to an electric dryer (240V) Easy DIY check

Electric dryers use 240V (two 120V legs). If one leg trips, the drum spins on the remaining 120V but the element gets no power — so no heat.

Test it yourself: Fully switch the dryer's double-pole breaker OFF, then back ON. Still no heat after that means the problem is inside the dryer. (A tester at the outlet showing only ~120V confirms a lost leg.)

🔩 Likely part: no part (breaker reset) — unless the breaker/outlet is faulty — $0

Tools: Voltage tester (optional)Time: 5 min

Cycling thermostat / high-limit thermostat Technician

These regulate and cap the temperature; a failed one can also cut the heat.

How a tech tests it: With power off, a tech tests each thermostat for continuity at room temperature (should read closed/continuity); open = replace. Usually checked alongside the thermal fuse.

🔩 Likely part: thermostat — $10–25

Tools: MultimeterTime: 30–60 min (tech)

Tools you'll need for the DIY checks

  • Multimeter (fuse, element, thermostat tests)
  • Vent brush + vacuum
  • Nut driver / screwdriver set
  • Voltage tester (optional, for the 240V check)

Repair or replace? (the honest math)

Fuses, thermostats, elements, and igniters are all cheap — typically $5–60 in parts — so a no-heat dryer is almost always worth fixing. Common questions

Why does my dryer run but not heat?

Almost always airflow or one heating part: a blocked vent that overheated and blew the thermal fuse, a burned-out element (electric) or weak igniter (gas), or a lost 240V leg. Clean the vent first.

How do I test a dryer’s thermal fuse?

Unplug the dryer and put a multimeter on the fuse — a good one reads continuity (near 0 ohms); an open (OL) reading means it's blown. Always clear the vent when you replace it.

My electric dryer spins but is stone cold — could it be the breaker?

Yes. Electric dryers need both 240V legs. Fully cycle the double-pole breaker off and on. If it's still cold, the issue is inside — usually the element or thermal fuse.

Is it the element or the igniter?

Element = electric dryers (test ~10–50 ohms; open = bad). Igniter = gas dryers (glows but won't light the burner). Tell us if it's gas or electric and we'll point you right.

Almost always — the parts are inexpensive. Send a 10-second video and we'll name the exact part and confirm it's worth it before you spend.
