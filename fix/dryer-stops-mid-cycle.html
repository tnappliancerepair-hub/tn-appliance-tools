My dryer stops in the middle of the cycle — what do I do?

A dryer that shuts off partway through — or stops and won't restart until it cools — is almost always protecting itself from heat or straining to run. The #1 cause is a restricted vent making it overheat, which is also a fire-safety issue, so that's where a tech starts. Here's the order.

⚠ Safety first: ⚠️ The most common cause is a restricted vent, which is a fire hazard. Clean the lint path before running more loads. Electric dryers run on 240V — internal parts (fuse, motor) are a tech repair with the dryer unplugged.

The most likely causes

Restricted vent causing overheating (check first) Moderate DIY check

The #1 cause. A clogged vent traps heat; the dryer's safety thermostat cuts the cycle when it overheats, so it stops mid-run (and often restarts once cooled). It's also the top cause of dryer fires.

Check it: Clean the lint screen, pull the dryer out and clear the duct behind it, and check the exterior vent flap. A full vent-run cleaning fixes the overheating and the fire risk — that's our dryer-vent cleaning service.

Failing thermal fuse or thermostat Technician

A weakening thermal fuse or cycling thermostat (often stressed by a past vent restriction) can cut power mid-cycle even when things aren't truly too hot.

Check it: If the vent is clean but it still stops early, a tech tests the thermal fuse and thermostats — affordable parts.

Worn motor overheating Technician

A tired drive motor can overheat and drop out mid-cycle, then run again once cooled. You may hear it hum or struggle before it quits.

Check it: A motor that stops when hot and runs when cool is a tech diagnosis.

Door switch or control glitch Moderate DIY check

A flaky door switch that intermittently reads "open," or a control fault, can stop the cycle unexpectedly.

Check it: Make sure the door latches firmly (a loose door can bounce the switch). If it still stops randomly, a tech checks the switch and control.

Is it worth fixing?

Repair — start with a vent cleaning (often the whole fix, and a safety must), and the heat-side/motor/switch parts are all affordable relative to a new dryer.

Common questions

Why does my dryer stop before the cycle is done?

Usually it's overheating from a clogged vent — the safety thermostat cuts the cycle to protect it, and it often restarts once cooled. Clean the full vent run first; it's the top cause and a fire risk.

Why does my dryer only run for a few minutes then stop?

Overheating from a restricted vent is the most common reason, followed by a failing thermal fuse/thermostat or a tired motor dropping out when hot. Clear the vent, then have the heat-side parts checked if it continues.

Is a dryer that stops mid-cycle dangerous?

The common cause — a clogged vent — is a genuine fire hazard, so yes, it's worth acting on promptly. Clear the vent; if it still stops, your $50 Quick Check credits toward the repair.
