My refrigerator is making a loud noise (buzzing, humming, or knocking) — what do I do?

A refrigerator makes some noise normally, but a NEW or loud sound — buzzing, humming, rattling, or knocking — usually means a fan or the compressor is working harder or wearing out. Where the sound comes from is the big clue: back-bottom (compressor area) vs inside the freezer (evaporator fan). Here's how a tech reads it, cheapest causes first.

⚠ Safety first: Low-risk. Unplug the fridge before pulling it out to reach the back-bottom components or removing an interior panel.

The most likely causes

Condenser fan or dirty coils (back-bottom hum/rattle) Easy DIY check

A common cause of a louder hum or rattle from the lower back: the condenser fan is dusty/obstructed or the coils are clogged, so it strains. This also makes the fridge run hot and long.

Check it: Unplug the fridge and vacuum the coils and condenser fan (behind the lower back panel or under the front). Clear any debris around the fan blade.

Evaporator fan (loud from inside the freezer) Moderate DIY check

If the noise is loudest from inside the freezer and changes when you open the door, the evaporator fan is likely worn — or its blade is hitting ice from a defrost problem.

Check it: Open the freezer: if the sound gets louder, it's the evap fan. A worn fan (or ice on the blade) is a tech diagnosis and affordable fix.

Compressor (steady hum/buzz from the back) Moderate DIY check

The compressor hums normally, but a louder buzz or a new rattle from it can mean it's straining (often from dirty coils) or the mounting is loose.

Check it: Clean the coils first — that quiets a straining compressor. A compressor that's genuinely loud or clicking on and off needs a tech.

Defrost timer or icemaker (periodic knock/click) Easy DIY check

A periodic knock, click, or short buzz can be the defrost timer cycling or the icemaker filling/harvesting — often normal, sometimes a sign of a sticking part.

Check it: Note if the sound matches the icemaker cycle or happens a few times a day (defrost). If it's truly disruptive or constant, have it checked.

Is it worth fixing?

Usually repair — most noises are free (clean the coils/fan) or an affordable fan motor. A genuinely failing compressor is the one costly case, which a tech can confirm before you decide.

Common questions

Why is my refrigerator so loud all of a sudden?

Most often a dusty condenser fan/coils making it strain (a hum/rattle from the back-bottom) or a worn evaporator fan (loud from inside the freezer). Cleaning the coils and fan is free and fixes a lot of cases.

Is a loud refrigerator dangerous?

Not dangerous, but a straining fan or compressor wastes power and wears out — and a fridge that's also warming needs attention. Start by cleaning the coils; if it stays loud or runs warm, have it looked at.
