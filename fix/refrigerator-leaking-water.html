My refrigerator is leaking water — what do I do?

Water on the floor or pooling under the crisper drawers has a short list of causes, and a couple are things you can safely check yourself. Here's how a technician narrows it down — from the cheapest, most common cause to the ones that need a service call.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the refrigerator before working near the drain or water line, and shut off the water supply if you disconnect the line.

The most likely causes

Clogged defrost drain Moderate DIY check

This is the #1 cause of water inside the fridge. The drain that carries defrost melt to the pan clogs with ice or gunk, so the water backs up and pools under the crispers or runs out the door.

Check it: Unplug the fridge, clear the drain hole at the back of the freezer/fresh-food floor, and flush it with warm water and a turkey baster. A recurring clog means a tech should add the factory drain-heater clip.

Leaking water supply line or inlet valve Moderate DIY check

The plastic or copper line feeding the dispenser/ice maker can crack or loosen at a fitting, or the inlet valve can seep — showing up as water at the back or under the unit.

Check it: Pull the fridge out, check the line and the shutoff behind it for drips and kinks, and make sure fittings are snug. A cracked line or a leaking valve is a tech replacement.

Clogged or frozen water filter housing Easy DIY check

A poorly seated, cracked, or wrong-model water filter can leak inside the housing, dripping down the inside of the fridge.

Check it: Reseat the filter or install a fresh correct-model one, and check the head for cracks.

Cracked or overflowing drain pan Moderate DIY check

Defrost water lands in a pan under the fridge to evaporate. A cracked pan, or one overwhelmed by a defrost problem, leaves puddles under the unit.

Check it: With the fridge unplugged and pulled out, inspect the drain pan underneath for cracks or overfilling.

Door not sealing / heavy condensation Easy DIY check

A worn door gasket or a door left ajar lets warm humid air in, which condenses and drips — often mistaken for a plumbing leak.

Check it: Check the gasket for gaps or tears and make sure the door closes flush and level.

Is it worth fixing?

Essentially always repair. Every common cause of a leaking fridge — a defrost drain, a water line, a filter, a gasket — is an inexpensive fix and never a reason to replace the appliance. The important part is diagnosing which one it is so it doesn't keep coming back.

Common questions

Why is my refrigerator leaking water on the floor?

Water on the floor is usually a leaking water supply line, a cracked or overflowing drain pan, or a clogged defrost drain backing up. Pull the fridge out and check the line and pan behind it first.

Why is there water pooling inside my fridge under the drawers?

Water pooling under the crisper drawers is the classic sign of a clogged defrost drain. Unplugging the fridge and flushing the drain at the back of the freezer floor with warm water clears most cases.
