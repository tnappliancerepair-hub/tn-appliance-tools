My microwave runs but won't heat — what do I do?

When a microwave lights up, spins the tray, and counts down but your food comes out cold, the cooking circuit has failed somewhere. A few of these are simple settings mistakes you can rule out in seconds; the rest are genuine tech repairs. One hard rule up front: never open the cabinet yourself.

⚠ Safety first: ⚠️ NEVER remove a microwave's outer cabinet. The high-voltage capacitor stores a dangerous — potentially lethal — charge even after it's unplugged. Settings checks are safe; anything inside the case is a trained tech only.

The most likely causes

It's in the wrong mode (power level / defrost / timer) Easy DIY check

A microwave set to a low power level, stuck in defrost, or running the kitchen timer instead of cook will spin and count down without heating. This is the #1 "not heating" call that turns out to be a setting.

Check it: Set power to 100%/High, cancel any defrost or timer, and heat one cup of water for one minute — if it's hot, the microwave is fine.

Failed magnetron Pro only DIY check

The magnetron is the tube that actually produces the microwaves. When it fails, everything else works — light, turntable, fan, timer — but there's no heat at all. It's the most common true failure.

Check it: No safe DIY test. If a full cup of water is still cold after a minute on High, this is the likely culprit — a tech confirms it and replaces the magnetron.

Bad high-voltage diode or capacitor Pro only DIY check

The diode and capacitor feed the magnetron. A failed diode often causes no-heat plus a loud buzz; these parts also hold that dangerous charge.

Check it: None — high-voltage components must be discharged and tested by a technician.

Faulty door switch Pro only DIY check

Microwaves have several interlock switches that must all confirm the door is shut before the unit will heat. One failed switch can let it run but block the cooking circuit.

Check it: Make sure nothing is stopping the door from latching fully; the switch replacement itself is a tech job (it's inside the case).

Is it worth fixing?

Over-the-range and built-in microwaves are almost always worth repairing — a magnetron or diode is an affordable part and replacing a mounted OTR unit is expensive. An inexpensive countertop microwave is usually a replace, since the repair can approach the cost of a new one.

Common questions

Is it worth fixing a microwave that won't heat?

For an over-the-range or built-in unit, yes — the parts are affordable and a new mounted microwave plus installation costs far more. For a cheap countertop model, replacing is often the better value.

Why does my microwave turn on but not get hot?

The turntable, light, and timer run off low voltage, but heat comes from the magnetron's high-voltage circuit. When the magnetron, diode, or a door interlock fails, everything looks normal but the food stays cold.

Can I fix a microwave myself?

You can rule out settings (power level, defrost, timer) safely. Do not open the cabinet — the capacitor holds a dangerous charge even unplugged. Internal repairs are a tech-only job. Your $50 Quick Check credits toward the repair.
