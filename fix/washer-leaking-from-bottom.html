My washer is leaking water from the bottom — what do I do?

Water on the floor under a washer looks alarming, but it usually traces to one of a few spots — and several are things you can check yourself. The key is finding where the water is actually coming from, because "leaking from the bottom" can start higher up and just run down. Here's the order a tech works it to pin the source.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the washer before pulling it out or reaching underneath, and have towels ready. Water and electricity don't mix — dry the area and the plug before restoring power.

The most likely causes

Too much detergent / oversudsing (check first) Easy DIY check

Extra suds overflow past seals and out the bottom — one of the most common "leaks" that isn't a broken part at all, especially with non-HE detergent in an HE machine.

Check it: Use only HE detergent in an HE washer, and cut the amount to about a tablespoon. Run a rinse cycle and watch — if the "leak" stops, that was it.

Loose or cracked hoses (fill or drain) Moderate DIY check

A very common real cause. The fill hoses at the back or the internal drain hose can loosen, crack, or lose a clamp, and the water runs down to the floor under the machine.

Check it: Check the fill-hose connections at the back are snug and the rubber washers are intact. Look for drips at the hose clamps. Tighten what's loose; a cracked hose is an inexpensive swap.

Clogged or leaking drain pump Technician

The pump moves the water out; a crack in the pump housing, a bad seal, or a coin/object stuck in it leaks right at the bottom during the drain.

Check it: On front-loaders, check the pump filter door at the lower front for trapped objects (have a pan ready). A cracked or seal-leaking pump is a tech replacement.

Door boot (front-loader) or tub seal Technician

On a front-loader, a torn rubber door boot leaks down the front and under the machine. A worn tub/bearing seal leaks from the center bottom during spin.

Check it: Inspect the rubber door boot for tears or trapped debris; wipe it and check the folds. A tub or bearing seal is an internal tech repair.

Overloaded or unbalanced load Easy DIY check

A packed or lopsided load pushes water over the tub during spin, which drips out the bottom — looks like a leak, isn't a broken part.

Check it: Wash smaller, balanced loads and see if the leak stops. Redistribute wet, heavy items so the tub isn't one-sided.

Is it worth fixing?

Usually repair — the most common causes (detergent, a loose or cracked hose, a load habit) are free or cheap fixes. Even a pump or door boot is an affordable part with flat labor, well below a new washer. Only a tub/bearing seal on an older machine is worth weighing against replacement.

Common questions

Why is my washer leaking from the bottom?

Most often it's too much detergent (oversudsing), a loose or cracked fill/drain hose, or the drain pump. On a front-loader, a torn door boot is common too. Start with the easy checks — cut detergent to a tablespoon of HE, and make sure the back hoses are snug — then a tech traces the rest.

Is a washer leaking from the bottom dangerous?

It's a water-damage and slip risk more than a shock risk, but unplug the washer before reaching underneath. Stop using it until you find the source so you're not repeatedly flooding the floor.

How much does it cost to fix a washer that leaks from the bottom?

Often free (detergent or a load habit) or an inexpensive hose. A pump or door boot is an affordable part with flat labor. Your $50 Quick Check credits toward the repair so you know the cost first.
