My dryer keeps tripping the breaker — what do I do?

Answered by Teddy Pivacek, owner & lead technician at TN Appliance Exchange — repairing appliances since 2012.

Quick answer: It's an electrical fault drawing too much current or shorting to ground. The most common cause is a shorted heating element (the dryer runs a few minutes, then trips), followed by a damaged cord/terminal block, a weak breaker, or a shorted motor.

A dryer that pops the breaker — sometimes right away, sometimes partway through a cycle — is telling you there's an electrical fault drawing too much current or shorting to ground. Some causes are simple, but this is a 240-volt appliance, so the checks stay conservative and the repair is a technician's job. Here's what's usually behind it.

⚠ Safety first: This is 240V. Unplug the dryer before touching anything, and never work inside the breaker panel yourself — panel and wiring issues are for a licensed electrician. If you smell burning or see scorching, stop and call a pro.

The most likely causes

Shorted heating element Technician

When the heating element coil breaks and the pieces touch the metal housing, it shorts to ground and trips the breaker — classically once the element heats up mid-cycle. This is the most common cause of a dryer tripping.

Check it: A dryer that runs a few minutes then trips, especially with heat, points here. The element is an affordable part, but testing and replacing it is a technician repair.

Damaged cord or terminal block Technician

A burnt, loose, or corroded connection at the power cord or the terminal block where it attaches can arc and trip the breaker.

Check it: With the dryer unplugged, a visibly scorched or melted cord/terminal is a clear sign. Replacement is a technician repair for safety.

Weak or failing breaker Technician

Breakers wear out and can start tripping below their rating, so a healthy dryer trips a tired breaker.

Check it: If the dryer tests fine, an aging breaker may be the cause — that's an electrician's call, not a dryer repair.

Shorted motor Technician

A failing drive motor can draw excessive current or short, tripping the breaker — often the dryer trips the instant it tries to start.

Check it: Tripping immediately at startup (before heating) can point at the motor. Diagnosed and replaced by a technician.

Lint-caused short or moisture Technician

Heavy lint buildup inside the cabinet, or moisture from a venting problem, can create a path that shorts and trips.

Check it: Keeping the vent clear and the interior free of packed lint helps — but locating an internal short is a technician job.

Is it worth fixing?

A heating element, cord, or terminal block is an affordable part, so a dryer that trips the breaker is usually worth repairing. Because it's a 240V electrical fault, the value is in a technician pinpointing the exact short safely rather than guessing at parts.

Common questions

Why does my dryer keep tripping the breaker?

It's an electrical fault drawing too much current or shorting to ground. The most common cause is a shorted heating element (the dryer runs a few minutes, then trips), followed by a damaged cord/terminal block, a weak breaker, or a shorted motor.

Is it safe to keep resetting a dryer that trips the breaker?

No. Repeatedly resetting a breaker that keeps tripping on the same circuit risks overheating and fire. Unplug the dryer and have the fault diagnosed. If you smell burning or see scorch marks, stop using it entirely until it's checked.

Can I fix a dryer that trips the breaker myself?

This one stays with a professional — it's a 240V circuit and the causes (shorted element, burnt terminal, failing motor, or a bad breaker) need proper testing. Never open your electrical panel yourself; that's a licensed electrician's work.
