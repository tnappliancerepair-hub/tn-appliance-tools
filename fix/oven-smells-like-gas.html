My oven or stove smells like gas — what do I do?

Answered by Teddy Pivacek, owner & lead technician at TN Appliance Exchange — repairing appliances since 2012.

Quick answer: If the smell is strong or persistent, treat it as an emergency: don't flip switches or light anything, get everyone out, and call your gas company's emergency line or 911 from outside. Only investigate a faint, brief smell once you're sure there's no active leak.

A gas smell needs a safety-first response before anything else — natural gas and propane are flammable, and a leak is not a repair you troubleshoot around. Read the safety steps first. Then, once you're safe, here are the real reasons a gas range can smell, from harmless to serious.

⚠ Safety first: STRONG or persistent gas smell = treat it as an emergency: do NOT flip any switch, light anything, or use your phone indoors. Get everyone out, leave the door open, and from outside or a neighbor's, call your gas company's emergency line or 911. Do not go back in until they say it's safe. Only troubleshoot a faint, brief smell once you're certain there's no active leak.

The most likely causes

Strong or steady gas smell — leak (EMERGENCY) Technician

A strong, persistent, or rotten-egg smell means gas is actively escaping and could ignite. This is never a DIY repair.

Check it: Leave the home now, don't touch switches or flames, and call the gas company's emergency line or 911 from outside. A technician or the utility locates and fixes the leak.

Burner not lighting (gas escaping) Technician

If a burner or the oven clicks or turns on but doesn't ignite, raw gas keeps flowing and you smell it.

Check it: Turn that burner/oven fully off, open windows to ventilate, and wait. Don't relight until the smell is gone. If it keeps failing to light, stop using it and get it serviced.

Brief smell right at ignition (often normal) Easy DIY check

A quick whiff of gas for a second or two as a burner lights, that then disappears, is often normal — a tiny bit of gas releases just before the flame catches.

Check it: If it's only a brief smell that clears the instant the flame lights and never lingers, that can be normal. Any smell that lingers or happens with no flame is not — shut it off.

Weak igniter letting gas flow (gas oven) Technician

A tired oven igniter can flow gas while it struggles to light, releasing gas before ignition — a common gas-oven issue that needs prompt service.

Check it: You may see the igniter glow with a delayed or no flame, and smell gas during that delay. Turn the oven off and have the igniter replaced — do not DIY gas parts.

Loose connection or gas valve Technician

A loose fitting at the range connection or a worn gas valve can leak gas at the appliance.

Check it: This is not a DIY fix. Shut off the gas to the range if you safely can, ventilate, and have a technician or the gas company inspect the connections and valve.

Is it worth fixing?

A gas leak is about safety, not repair-vs-replace — get it made safe first. Once the leak is resolved, the underlying part (an igniter, a valve, a connection) is usually an affordable, worthwhile repair by a qualified technician. We never cut corners on gas.

Common questions

What should I do if my stove smells like gas?

If the smell is strong or persistent, treat it as an emergency: don't flip switches or light anything, get everyone out, and call your gas company's emergency line or 911 from outside. Only investigate a faint, brief smell once you're sure there's no active leak.

Is it normal to smell gas when lighting my stove?

A brief whiff for a second or two as the burner lights, that clears the instant the flame catches, can be normal. A smell that lingers, happens without a flame, or is strong is not normal — turn it off, ventilate, and get it checked.

Can I fix a gas smell from my oven myself?

No — gas leaks and gas components (igniters, valves, connections) are not DIY repairs. Make the situation safe first (ventilate, call the gas company or 911 if it's strong), then have a qualified technician diagnose and fix the underlying part.
