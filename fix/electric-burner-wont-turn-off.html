My electric stove burner won't turn off — what do I do?

Answered by Teddy Pivacek, owner & lead technician at TN Appliance Exchange — repairing appliances since 2012.

Quick answer: Yes. A burner stuck on with continuous power is a fire and burn risk. Turn off the range's breaker immediately to cut power to it, keep flammables away, and don't use it until the stuck part is replaced.

A burner that stays hot no matter what you do with the knob is a genuine safety issue, not a wait-and-see. The good news is the cause is almost always one affordable, well-understood part — but the first thing to do is cut the power so it can't start a fire. Here's what's happening and what comes next.

⚠ Safety first: Turn off the range's breaker right now to stop the stuck burner from heating — don't just rely on the knob. Keep anything flammable away from the cooktop until power is off. Do not attempt internal repairs on a live 240V range.

The most likely causes

Welded (stuck) infinite switch Technician

The knob's "infinite switch" cycles the burner on and off to set the heat. Its contacts can weld shut from arcing over time, so the burner gets continuous power and stays on high regardless of the knob. This is the #1 cause.

Check it: Confirm it by killing the breaker (the burner should cool). The switch is an affordable part, but replacing it inside a 240V range is a technician repair.

Element shorted to the switch Technician

A coil element that shorts internally can feed power back so it stays energized. Less common than the switch, but it presents the same "won't turn off" symptom.

Check it: Not a DIY diagnosis. A technician tests whether it's the switch or the element — both are affordable parts.

Control board fault (smooth-top / electronic ranges) Technician

On electronic-control ranges, a relay on the control board that switches the element can weld closed, keeping the element powered — the same failure mode as an infinite switch, on the board.

Check it: Kill the breaker to make it safe. A stuck relay on the control board is a technician repair.

Wiring fault Technician

Rarely, damaged or shorted wiring behind a burner keeps it energized.

Check it: Not DIY on a 240V appliance. A technician locates and repairs the fault safely.

Is it worth fixing?

The usual cause — an infinite switch — is an inexpensive part, and even a control-board relay is a worthwhile fix, so a stuck burner is almost always repairable. The value is in a technician safely confirming which part welded, because this is a 240V circuit and a fire risk if left running.

Common questions

My electric burner won't turn off — is it dangerous?

Yes. A burner stuck on with continuous power is a fire and burn risk. Turn off the range's breaker immediately to cut power to it, keep flammables away, and don't use it until the stuck part is replaced.

Why won't my stove burner turn off with the knob?

Almost always a welded infinite switch — the knob's switch that's supposed to cycle the burner on and off has its contacts fused shut, so the element gets constant power. On electronic ranges, a stuck relay on the control board does the same thing.

Can I fix a burner that won't turn off myself?

Make it safe yourself by shutting off the breaker — but the repair (an infinite switch, element, or control board inside a 240V range) is a technician job for safety. The parts are affordable, so it's well worth fixing.
