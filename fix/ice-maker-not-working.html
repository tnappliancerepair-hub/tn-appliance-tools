My ice maker isn't working — what do I do?

A dead ice maker is the single most common refrigerator complaint we see. The good news: the causes are a short, well-understood list, and several of them you can check safely yourself before calling anyone. Here they are in the order a technician works through them.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the refrigerator before removing the ice maker or working near the water valve, and shut off the water supply if you disconnect any line.

The most likely causes

Frozen water fill tube Easy DIY check

The small tube that fills the ice mold can freeze shut, usually because the freezer runs a touch too cold or a prior fill dribbled and froze. Water can't reach the mold, so no ice.

Check it: Unplug the fridge, find the fill tube at the back of the ice maker, and gently thaw the ice plug with a hair dryer on low or a cup of warm water. If ice returns after a few days, the underlying cause needs a tech.

Clogged or overdue water filter Easy DIY check

A water filter that's past due chokes water flow to the ice maker (and dispenser). Low flow means small cubes, hollow cubes, or no ice at all.

Check it: Replace the water filter if it's more than ~6 months old and see if flow returns. This is the cheapest thing to rule out first.

Failed water inlet valve Moderate DIY check

The inlet valve opens to let water into the ice maker. It needs adequate household water pressure to work; when it fails or pressure is low, the mold never fills.

Check it: Check that the saddle valve or shutoff behind/under the fridge is fully open and the line isn't kinked. The valve replacement itself is a tech job.

Ice maker module / motor failure Moderate DIY check

The ice maker head contains a small motor and control that cycle the ejector arm and dump the cubes. When the module fails, the maker won't harvest even with water present.

Check it: Confirm the ice maker is switched on and the shutoff arm/paddle isn't stuck in the up (off) position. A module swap is a tech job but an affordable, common one.

Stuck shutoff arm or control switch Easy DIY check

The wire shutoff arm (or on newer units, an optical/lever sensor) stops ice when the bin is full. If it's stuck up or the sensor is dirty, the maker thinks the bin is always full.

Check it: Make sure the arm is down and moves freely, and clear any ice chunks jamming the bin or sensor.

Is it worth fixing?

Almost always repair. Fill-tube thaws, filters, inlet valves, and ice maker modules are inexpensive relative to the fridge, and none of them are a reason to replace the appliance. We'll pinpoint the exact part so you're not throwing money at guesses.

Common questions

Why did my ice maker suddenly stop making ice?

The most common sudden causes are a frozen fill tube, a water filter that's overdue, or a failed water inlet valve. Start by checking that the ice maker is switched on, the shutoff arm is down, and the filter isn't past due.

Can I fix my ice maker myself?

You can safely thaw a frozen fill tube, replace the water filter, confirm the water shutoff is open, and make sure the shutoff arm isn't stuck. Valve and module replacements are quick tech jobs — send us your model number and we'll bring the right part.

How much does ice maker repair cost?
