My top-load washer fills but won't agitate — what do I do?

Answered by Teddy Pivacek, owner & lead technician at TN Appliance Exchange — repairing appliances since 2012.

Quick answer: The motor is running but the part that converts its power to agitation has failed — most often worn agitator dogs (the top spins freely), a broken motor coupler on direct-drive models, or a worn drive belt. All are common, affordable fixes.

When a top-loader fills with water and the timer advances but the agitator sits still (or barely twists), the problem is in the parts that turn the motor's power into that back-and-forth motion. Several of these are common, affordable fixes. Here's how we sort out which one you've got.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the washer before checking anything internal. The agitator and drive components carry moving-part risk when powered.

The most likely causes

Worn agitator dogs (directional cogs) Moderate DIY check

On many top-loaders the agitator has small plastic "dogs" that grab in one direction to drive it. When they wear smooth, the top of the agitator spins freely but doesn't move the clothes. Very common and cheap.

Check it: Grab the agitator and twist it back and forth — if the top ratchets or turns loosely without engaging, the dogs are worn. The agitator-dog kit is an inexpensive part and a common DIY.

Motor coupler (direct-drive models) Technician

On direct-drive washers a plastic-and-rubber coupler connects the motor to the transmission. It's designed to fail before more expensive parts, so it wears or breaks and the agitator stops — often you'll hear the motor run with no motion.

Check it: A running motor with no agitation, sometimes with a burnt-rubber smell, points here. It's an affordable part; replacement is a moderate repair.

Broken or slipped drive belt (belt-drive models) Technician

On belt-driven washers a worn or broken belt means the motor spins but the transmission doesn't.

Check it: If the motor hums or runs but nothing moves, and the machine is belt-driven, the belt is a likely and affordable cause. Technician repair.

Lid switch or control Technician

If the washer doesn't sense the lid closed, it may fill but refuse to agitate or spin.

Check it: Make sure the lid closes firmly and the switch clicks. A failed lid switch is an affordable technician repair.

Transmission or drive block Technician

Less commonly, a worn transmission or a stripped drive block under the agitator stops the motion.

Check it: Not a DIY diagnosis. A technician confirms whether it's the affordable coupler/dogs or the transmission.

Is it worth fixing?

Agitator dogs, a motor coupler, or a belt are all inexpensive parts, and the labor is routine — a washer that won't agitate is well worth repairing on most machines. Only a failed transmission on an older washer tips the math toward replacement, and we'll tell you straight when it does.

Common questions

Why does my washer fill with water but not agitate?

The motor is running but the part that converts its power to agitation has failed — most often worn agitator dogs (the top spins freely), a broken motor coupler on direct-drive models, or a worn drive belt. All are common, affordable fixes.

How do I know if my agitator dogs are worn?

With the washer empty, grab the top of the agitator and twist it back and forth. If it ratchets or turns loosely without driving the lower agitator, the directional cogs (dogs) are worn out. The replacement kit is inexpensive.

Is a washer that won't agitate worth fixing?

Usually yes — agitator dogs, a motor coupler, or a belt are cheap parts and routine repairs. The only time replacement makes sense is a failed transmission on an older machine, and we give you honest numbers before you decide.
