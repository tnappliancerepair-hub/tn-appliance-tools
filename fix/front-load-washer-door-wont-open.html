My front-load washer door won't open — what do I do?

A front-load washer keeps the door locked any time there's water in the tub or a cycle is running — it's a flood-prevention design. So a door that won't open is usually the machine telling you either water is still inside or the door lock didn't release. Most of the time you can get it open yourself. Here's the order a tech works it.

⚠ Safety first: Don't force the door — you can break the handle or latch. If there's water in the tub, drain it first (below) so you're not dumping a flood on the floor. Unplug the washer before reaching into the drain filter.

The most likely causes

Water still in the tub (check first) Moderate DIY check

The lock stays engaged until the water is drained. A cycle that stopped mid-wash, or a drain problem, leaves water in and the door locked.

Check it: Run a drain/spin cycle to pump it out. If it won't drain, open the small filter door at the lower front, have a pan and towels ready, and slowly open the drain filter to let the water out — that usually releases the lock.

Cycle still running or just finished Easy DIY check

The door stays locked during the whole cycle and for a minute or two after, while the control confirms it's safe.

Check it: Wait 2–3 minutes after the cycle ends. Many washers show a "locked" light that goes out when it's safe to open.

Control glitch — needs a reset Easy DIY check

A glitch can leave the lock engaged even when the tub is empty and the cycle is done.

Check it: Unplug the washer (or flip its breaker) for 5 minutes, then restore power. The reset re-runs the lock and usually releases the door.

Failed door lock assembly Technician

The real mechanical cause. The door lock has a small latch and switch that wear out; when it fails it can stay locked (and often throws a lock-related code).

Check it: Many front-loaders have a manual-release tab or string behind the lower filter door — check your model. If the tub is empty, it's reset, and it's still locked, the lock assembly is the fix a tech replaces.

Is it worth fixing?

Usually repair — most stuck doors are just water or a reset (free). If the door lock assembly failed, it's an affordable, common part with flat labor, well below a new washer.

Common questions

How do I open a front-load washer door that's stuck?

First make sure the tub is empty — the door stays locked whenever there's water in it. Run a drain/spin, or if it won't drain, open the lower-front filter door (with a pan ready) and drain it manually; that usually releases the lock. If it's empty and still stuck, unplug the washer for 5 minutes to reset it. Many models also have a manual-release tab behind the filter door. Never force it.

Why is my washer door locked when the cycle is done?

It stays locked for a couple minutes after a cycle, and it won't open at all if any water is left in the tub. If it's empty, done, and a reset didn't free it, the door lock assembly has likely failed — an affordable, common repair.

Yes — it's often free (water or a reset), and even a failed door lock is a cheap common part. Your $50 Quick Check credits toward the repair so you know the cost first.
