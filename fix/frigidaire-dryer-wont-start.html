Frigidaire Dryer Won't Start?

A Frigidaire (or Electrolux) dryer that won't start is most often the door switch, a worn start switch, or a blown thermal fuse from a clogged vent. Start with the free checks — door latched firmly and Control Lock off — before assuming a part.
The known Frigidaire issue: A Frigidaire dryer that hums or does nothing when you press Start — with the door clearly shut and Control Lock off — is usually the door switch or start switch, both affordable parts. And any "won't start" tied to overheating means a clogged vent blew the thermal fuse: clean the vent or it happens again.

What's actually different about a Frigidaire

Door not latched / door switch (check first) Moderate

If the door switch doesn't sense the door closed, the dryer won't start.
What you can check: Close the door firmly and listen for the click; a loose door or no click points at the latch/door switch.

Control Lock is on Easy

Control Lock disables the buttons.
What you can check: Hold the lock button (~3 sec) to clear it, then start.

Blown thermal fuse (clogged vent) Pro

A clogged vent overheats the dryer and blows the thermal fuse, which can cut the motor so it won't start.
What you can check: Clean the entire vent line (the root cause); the fuse is an affordable part but re-blows if the vent stays clogged.

Start switch / control Pro

A worn push-to-start switch or the main control can stop it responding.
What you can check: If door, lock, and vent are all good, a tech checks the start switch and control.
Try this first: Clear Control Lock (hold the lock button ~3 sec), shut the door firmly, and hold Start. Unplug 5 minutes to clear a control glitch.

How this failure works on any dryer

A dryer that won't turn on — no hum, no tumble — comes down to a handful of causes, and a couple are things you can rule out in two minutes before calling anyone. Here's the order a technician checks, from the free stuff to the parts that need a service call.

Safety first: Unplug the dryer (or switch off its breaker) before opening any panel. A dryer runs on 240V — never poke inside a plugged-in machine.

Power / tripped breaker Easy

An electric dryer uses a double breaker; if half trips, the dryer can go completely dead or run without heat. A loose cord or dead outlet does the same.
What you can check: Fully flip the dryer's breaker off and back on, and confirm the cord is seated. This is the free first check that fixes a surprising number of "dead" dryers.

Failed door switch Moderate

The door switch tells the dryer the door is closed. If it fails, the dryer won't start no matter what — and it's one of the most common no-start causes.
What you can check: Press the door firmly shut and listen for the click when you open/close it. No click can mean a bad switch. Replacement is a quick tech job.

Blown thermal fuse Moderate

A thermal fuse blows to protect the dryer from overheating — usually caused by a clogged vent. On many models a blown fuse kills the start circuit entirely, so the dryer is dead.
What you can check: A blown thermal fuse almost always means your vent is restricted. Clean the full vent line first; a tech replaces the fuse and confirms airflow so it doesn't blow again.

Failed start switch or timer/control Moderate

The push-to-start switch or the control board can fail so nothing happens when you press start, even with power and a good door switch.
What you can check: Listen for a faint hum when you hold start — a totally silent press points at the start switch or control. This is a tech diagnosis and repair.

Broken belt (with belt switch) Moderate

Some dryers have a belt switch that prevents starting if the belt has snapped, so a broken belt can look like a no-start. You'll usually have heard a thump or the drum will spin freely by hand.
What you can check: With the dryer unplugged, reach in and turn the drum — if it spins with no resistance, the belt is likely broken. Belt replacement is a tech job.

Is it worth repairing?

Almost always repair. Door switches, thermal fuses, start switches, and belts are all affordable parts, and a no-start dryer is one of the most economical repairs we do. Replacement only enters the picture if it's an old machine stacking several failures at once.

Why won't my dryer turn on at all?

A completely dead dryer is usually a power/breaker issue or a failed door switch; a dryer that hums but won't tumble is often the start switch or a broken belt. Reset the breaker and check for the door-switch click first.

Can I fix a dryer that won't start myself?

You can safely reset the breaker, reseat the cord, confirm the door clicks shut, and check the vent for a restriction (the usual cause of a blown thermal fuse). Switch, fuse, and belt replacements are quick tech jobs — unplug it first, it's 240V.
