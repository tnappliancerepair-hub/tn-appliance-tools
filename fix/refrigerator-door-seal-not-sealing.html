My refrigerator door won't seal — what do I do?

Answered by Teddy Pivacek, owner & lead technician at TN Appliance Exchange — repairing appliances since 2012.

Quick answer: Close the door on a dollar bill and pull it out. If it slides free with no resistance, the seal is weak at that spot — check all the way around. Condensation on the door, a fridge that runs constantly, or a visibly cracked gasket are other signs.

A door that won't seal makes the fridge run non-stop, sweat with condensation, and let food warm up. The good news: the fix is usually a cheap gasket or a simple alignment, not a new refrigerator. Here's how we find out why the seal is failing and what you can check right now.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the refrigerator before removing a gasket or adjusting hinges. Have towels handy for any condensation that has built up.

The most likely causes

Dirty or sticky gasket Easy DIY check

Syrup, grease, and grime on the rubber gasket keep it from making clean contact, or make it stick and tear when the door opens. This is the most common and easiest cause.

Check it: Wipe the gasket and the surface it seals against with warm soapy water, dry it, and wipe a thin film of petroleum jelly on the hinge side so it flexes instead of tearing.

Torn, cracked, or deformed gasket Moderate DIY check

Over years the rubber hardens, cracks, or takes a permanent set (often from being left open or from heat), so it no longer springs against the frame.

Check it: Do the dollar-bill test: close the door on a bill and pull — if it slides out with no drag anywhere, the seal is weak there. A gasket is an affordable replacement part.

Door sagging or out of alignment Moderate DIY check

A worn hinge or a heavy door loaded with condiments can drop the door slightly so the seal no longer meets the frame evenly.

Check it: Look at the gaps around the closed door — an uneven gap top-to-bottom points at a sagging door. Hinge adjustment or replacement squares it up.

Overpacked shelves or door bins Easy DIY check

Items pushed too far forward, or overloaded door shelves, can physically hold the door open a crack.

Check it: Make sure nothing on the shelves or in the door bins is poking past the seal line and holding the door open.

Warped door or cabinet Technician

Rarely, the door liner or cabinet warps so the sealing surfaces no longer match up.

Check it: Not a DIY fix. A technician confirms whether it's the door or the frame and what's worth doing.

Is it worth fixing?

A clean-and-condition is free, and a replacement gasket is an inexpensive part that makes an old fridge seal like new — well worth it, since a bad seal drives up your power bill and can spoil food. Replacement only enters the picture if the door or cabinet itself is warped.

Common questions

How do I know if my refrigerator door seal is bad?

Close the door on a dollar bill and pull it out. If it slides free with no resistance, the seal is weak at that spot — check all the way around. Condensation on the door, a fridge that runs constantly, or a visibly cracked gasket are other signs.

Can I fix a refrigerator door that won't seal myself?

Often yes — cleaning and conditioning the gasket, making sure nothing is blocking the door, and checking the door alignment are all safe. If the gasket is torn or the door is sagging from a worn hinge, that's an affordable part and repair.

Is a bad refrigerator door seal worth fixing?

Definitely. A gasket is a cheap part, and a failing seal makes the fridge run non-stop, raises your electric bill, and risks food safety. Fixing the seal is almost always worth it over replacing the fridge.
