Samsung Dryer Not Heating?

A Samsung dryer that tumbles but won't heat is almost always the heating element or a thermal fuse that blew because the vent is clogged. The element (part family DC47-00019A) is a common, affordable replacement — but if you don't clear the vent that blew the fuse, the fix won't last.
The known Samsung issue: Samsung dryer heating elements and thermal fuses are common, inexpensive parts — the repair is well worth it. Always clear the vent at the same time or the new fuse will blow again.

How this failure works on any dryer

When a dryer tumbles fine but everything comes out cold and damp, the problem is in the heat circuit — not the motor. Nine times out of ten it traces back to one of a few affordable parts, and very often the root cause is a clogged vent that overheated and tripped a safety device. Here is exactly what we check, and the safe things you can look at first.

Safety first: Unplug an electric dryer before checking anything internal. For GAS dryers, do not attempt igniter or gas-valve work yourself — that is a call-a-pro repair.

Clogged vent (the hidden root cause) Easy

A restricted vent traps heat inside the dryer and trips the thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat as a safety measure. Fix the part without clearing the vent and it just fails again. It's also the #1 dryer fire risk.
What you can check: Disconnect and clear the vent hose and exhaust duct. If drying has been slow for weeks, this is very likely part of your problem.

Heating element Pro

The element produces the heat. On electric dryers it burns out over time and the dryer runs cold. This is a common, affordable part.
What you can check: No safe DIY test without a meter and disassembly — but a dryer that tumbles normally with zero heat often points here.

Blown thermal fuse Pro

A one-time safety fuse that cuts heat (or all power, depending on model) when the dryer overheats — usually because of that clogged vent.
What you can check: Clear the vent first. The fuse itself is a technician replacement, and it must be paired with fixing the airflow that blew it.

Thermal cutoff / high-limit thermostat Pro

These regulate and cap the temperature. When they fail, heat can drop out.
What you can check: Not a DIY check. Diagnosed and replaced by a technician.

Lost 240V leg (electric dryers) Easy

Electric dryers use two 120V legs. If one breaker leg trips or fails, the motor still runs on one leg but the element gets no power — so it tumbles but won't heat.
What you can check: Check your breaker: fully switch the dryer's double breaker off and back on. If a leg is lost at the panel, that's an electrical issue.

Gas igniter or flame sensor (gas dryers) Pro

On gas models a weak igniter or bad flame sensor stops the burner from lighting, so no heat.
What you can check: Do not DIY gas components. This is a technician repair for safety.

Is it worth repairing?

Heating elements, fuses, thermostats, and igniters are all affordable parts, and the labor is straightforward — a no-heat dryer is well worth repairing in almost every case. Clearing the vent at the same time is what keeps the fix from failing again.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my Samsung dryer not heating?

A Samsung dryer that tumbles but won't heat is almost always the heating element or a thermal fuse that blew because the vent is clogged. The element (part family DC47-00019A) is a common, affordable replacement — but if you don't clear the vent that blew the fuse, the fix won't last.

Is there a known issue with Samsung dryers not heating?

Samsung dryer heating elements and thermal fuses are common, inexpensive parts — the repair is well worth it. Always clear the vent at the same time or the new fuse will blow again.

Why does my dryer run but not get hot?

The motor and the heat circuit are separate. If it tumbles but stays cold, the issue is in the heat side — most often a heating element, a thermal fuse blown by a clogged vent, or (on electric dryers) a lost 240V breaker leg.

Can a clogged vent stop a dryer from heating?

Yes. A restricted vent traps heat and trips the thermal fuse or high-limit thermostat as a safety response, cutting the heat. Clearing the vent is the first thing to do — and it prevents a fire hazard.

Is it worth fixing a dryer that won't heat?

Usually yes. The common causes are inexpensive parts and a quick repair. Replacement only makes sense if the dryer has other major problems on top of the no-heat issue.
