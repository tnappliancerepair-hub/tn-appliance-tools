My refrigerator is warm but the freezer is cold — what do I do?

When the freezer stays cold but the fridge section is warm, it's a very specific and common clue: the cold is being made fine, but it's not getting moved into the fridge. On most refrigerators both sections are cooled by one evaporator in the freezer, and a fan blows that cold air up into the fridge. So a warm-fridge/cold-freezer almost always points at airflow — a defrost problem or the fan. Here's the order a tech works it.

⚠ Safety first: Low-risk checks. Unplug the fridge before removing the freezer back panel to inspect the coils or fan. Move perishables to a cooler if the fridge has been warm for a while.

The most likely causes

Frost blocking the evaporator (defrost problem) Moderate DIY check

The #1 cause. If the auto-defrost system fails, frost builds up on the evaporator coils in the freezer and blocks the air path, so the fan can't push cold air into the fridge — freezer still cold, fridge warms up.

Check it: A quick test: unplug the fridge for 24 hours to let it fully defrost, then plug back in. If the fridge cools again for a day or two then warms up, that confirms a defrost fault (heater, sensor, or timer/control) — a tech repair.

Failed evaporator fan Technician

The fan in the freezer that blows cold air into the fridge can fail. The freezer stays cold by conduction, but no air moves to the fridge. Often you'll hear no fan, or a clicking/buzzing.

Check it: Open the freezer and listen for the fan. Silence (or a fan that stops when you press the door switch by hand) points to a failed evaporator fan — an affordable, common part a tech replaces.

Blocked vents between freezer and fridge Easy DIY check

The vents that pass cold air into the fridge can be blocked by packed food or ice, choking the airflow.

Check it: Make sure food isn't packed against the vents (usually the upper back of the fridge section). Clear any ice you can see around the vents.

Damper or control fault Technician

A stuck air damper (which meters cold air into the fridge) or a control problem can leave the fridge under-cooled while the freezer runs.

Check it: If the coils are clear and the fan runs, a tech checks the damper and control.

Is it worth fixing?

Usually repair — a defrost part or an evaporator fan is an affordable, common repair with flat labor, well below a new fridge. The 24-hour defrost test is free and often tells you (and the tech) exactly where to look.

Common questions

Why is my refrigerator warm but the freezer cold?

Because both sections are usually cooled by one set of coils in the freezer, with a fan moving that cold air into the fridge. If the fridge is warm but the freezer's fine, the cold isn't getting moved — almost always frost blocking the coils (a defrost fault) or a failed evaporator fan. Both are common, affordable repairs.

How do I fix a fridge that's warm but the freezer is cold?

Try the free test first: unplug the fridge for 24 hours to fully defrost, then restore power. If it cools again for a day or two then warms up, it's a defrost fault (heater/sensor/control). If you also hear no fan in the freezer, it's likely the evaporator fan. Both are tech repairs on affordable common parts.
