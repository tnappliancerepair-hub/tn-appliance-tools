My washer won't fill with water — what do I do?

A washer that runs but never fills — or fills painfully slowly — has a break somewhere between your house water and the tub. The good news is the first few causes are things you can check yourself in a couple minutes, and they're free. Here's the order a tech works it.

⚠ Safety first: Unplug the washer before pulling it out or disconnecting hoses, and have towels ready — a little water comes out of the hoses. Turn the water valves off before removing a fill hose.

The most likely causes

Water valves not fully open (check first) Easy DIY check

The two hot/cold valves behind the washer get bumped or were left partly closed — the #1 free cause of no-fill or slow-fill.

Check it: Make sure both the hot and cold supply valves behind the washer are turned all the way on. A half-open valve fills slowly or trips a "long fill" error.

Kinked or crushed fill hose Easy DIY check

The fill hose behind the washer gets kinked when the machine is pushed back, choking the water flow.

Check it: Pull the washer out a little and straighten the fill hoses so nothing is kinked or crushed behind it.

Clogged inlet screens Moderate DIY check

There are small mesh screens where the fill hoses connect to the washer; sediment and mineral buildup clog them and starve the fill.

Check it: Turn off the valves, unscrew the fill hoses at the washer, and check the little screens in the inlet ports. Rinse out any grit or scale, then reconnect. This fixes a lot of slow-fill cases.

Failed water inlet valve Technician

The inlet valve is the electric valve that lets water into the washer on command. When it fails, the washer calls for water and nothing comes in.

Check it: If the supply valves are open, the hoses are clear, and the screens are clean but it still won't fill, the inlet valve is the usual fix — an affordable, common tech repair.

Lid switch or control (won't start the fill) Technician

On some machines a bad lid switch or control keeps the cycle from advancing to fill at all.

Check it: If the washer won't even begin filling and the lid latches fine, a tech checks the lid switch and control.

Is it worth fixing?

Usually repair — most no-fill is free (open the valves, straighten the hose, clean the screens). If it's the water inlet valve, that's an affordable, common part with flat labor, well below a new washer.

Common questions

Why won't my washer fill with water?

Most often the supply valves behind it aren't fully open, the fill hose is kinked, or the small inlet screens are clogged with sediment — all free to fix. If the valves are open, the hoses are clear, and the screens are clean but it still won't fill, the water inlet valve has failed and gets replaced, an affordable common part.

How do I fix a washer that fills slowly?

Slow fill is usually a half-open supply valve or clogged inlet screens. Open both valves all the way, then turn them off, unscrew the fill hoses at the washer, and rinse the little mesh screens in the inlet ports. That fixes most slow-fill cases.

Is a washer that won't fill worth fixing?

Yes — it's often free (a valve or a screen) or an affordable inlet valve. Your $50 Quick Check credits toward the repair so you know the cost first.
