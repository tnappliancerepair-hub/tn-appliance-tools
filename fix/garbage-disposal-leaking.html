My garbage disposal is leaking — what do I do?

Answered by Teddy Pivacek, owner & lead technician at TN Appliance Exchange — repairing appliances since 2012.

Quick answer: It depends where. A leak from the top is the sink flange seal, a leak from the side is a hose or drain connection, and a leak from the bottom means the internal seals have failed and the disposal needs replacing. Dry it, run water, and watch where it drips.

A leaking garbage disposal is one of those problems where finding exactly where the water comes from tells you the whole story. Top, side, or bottom each points at a different, specific fix — and only one of them means the disposal itself is finished. Here's how to pin it down safely.

⚠ Safety first: Turn off the disposal at the wall switch AND flip its breaker before reaching underneath — never put a hand near the disposal with power on. Dry the unit, then run a little water to see exactly where it drips from.

The most likely causes

Leak from the TOP — sink flange Moderate DIY check

If water leaks where the disposal meets the sink drain, the plumber's putty or the mounting flange seal has dried out and failed. Common with age.

Check it: Dry everything, run water, and watch the top rim where it mounts to the sink. A top leak usually means the flange needs re-sealing (fresh putty and re-tightened mounts) — a moderate DIY or a quick plumber/technician job.

Leak from the SIDE — hose connections Moderate DIY check

The two side connections — the dishwasher inlet hose and the main discharge drain pipe — have gaskets and clamps that loosen or crack.

Check it: Watch the two side connections while water runs. Tighten a loose clamp or the discharge flange bolts; replace a cracked gasket. Straightforward once you see which fitting drips.

Leak from the BOTTOM — internal seal failed Technician

Water dripping from the very bottom (the reset-button end) means the internal seals inside the disposal have failed and water is getting into the motor housing. That's the end of that disposal.

Check it: A bottom leak is not repairable — the disposal needs replacing. Don't keep running it, since water in the motor is a shock and failure risk.

Dishwasher backing up through the disposal Moderate DIY check

A clog at the disposal's dishwasher inlet can push water back and look like a disposal leak during a dishwasher cycle.

Check it: If the leak only happens when the dishwasher runs, check the dishwasher hose connection and the disposal inlet for a clog.

Is it worth fixing?

A top or side leak is a cheap re-seal or a clamp/gasket — well worth fixing. A bottom leak means the disposal's internal seals are gone and it should be replaced rather than repaired; disposals are an affordable unit to swap. Finding where it leaks tells you which situation you're in.

Common questions

Why is my garbage disposal leaking?

It depends where. A leak from the top is the sink flange seal, a leak from the side is a hose or drain connection, and a leak from the bottom means the internal seals have failed and the disposal needs replacing. Dry it, run water, and watch where it drips.

Can I fix a leaking garbage disposal myself?

A top-flange re-seal or tightening a side hose connection is a doable DIY (with the power OFF at the switch and breaker). A bottom leak isn't repairable — that disposal is done and should be replaced.

Is a leaking garbage disposal worth fixing?

A top or side leak is a cheap seal or clamp and worth fixing. A bottom leak means replacing the unit — but disposals are inexpensive to swap, so either way it's an affordable repair.
