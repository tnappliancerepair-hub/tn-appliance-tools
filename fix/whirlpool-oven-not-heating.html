Whirlpool Oven Not Heating?

Whirlpool (and Maytag / KitchenAid) ovens that won't heat are usually the simplest fix in the business on electric models — a burned-out bake element you can often see. On gas models it's the igniter. Rule out the control lock and settings first.
The known Whirlpool issue: A Whirlpool electric oven that won't heat is a burned-out bake element more often than anything else — and you can usually see the damage. It's one of the cheapest, most satisfying oven repairs there is.

What's actually different about a Whirlpool

Burned-out bake element (electric — #1) Pro

The lower bake element is the classic Whirlpool no-heat — it burns through, often with a visible blister or break, and the oven won't heat or won't reach temp.
What you can check: With the oven cool, look at the bake element for a broken/blistered spot. A visibly bad element is an affordable, common replacement.

Weak igniter (gas) Pro

The gas igniter weakens until it can't open the gas valve — glows but no flame.
What you can check: Igniter glows but no flame = worn igniter, a common affordable part.

Control lock or wrong setting (check first) Easy

Control lock, a timer/delay-bake set, or Sabbath mode can make it seem like the oven won't heat.
What you can check: Make sure control lock and Sabbath mode are off and no delayed bake is set. Free to rule out.

Control board or sensor (F3E1) Pro

A failed control relay won't power the element; a shorted oven sensor (F3E1) blocks heating.
What you can check: If the element/igniter and settings are good, a tech checks the control and sensor.

How this failure works on any oven

An oven that won't come up to temperature splits cleanly by fuel type: on electric ovens it's almost always the bake element, and on gas ovens it's almost always the igniter. Both are common, affordable repairs. Here's how to tell what you're dealing with, and the safe checks first.

Safety first: For GAS ovens, do not attempt igniter or gas-valve work yourself — call a technician. For electric ovens, cut power at the breaker before inspecting anything.

Bake element (electric ovens) Moderate

The bottom heating element is the most common electric-oven failure. A failed element often has a visible blister, break, or burn spot and won't glow.
What you can check: Look at the element with the oven cool: a visible break or burn mark is a strong sign. Replacement is a straightforward repair.

Oven igniter (gas ovens) Pro

The igniter both lights the gas and tells the valve to open. As it weakens with age it glows but no longer gets hot enough to open the valve — so the oven doesn't heat. This is the #1 gas-oven failure.
What you can check: You may see the igniter glow but no flame. Do not DIY gas parts — this is a technician repair for safety.

Temperature sensor Pro

The oven's temperature sensor tells the control how hot it is. A failed sensor can cause no-heat or wildly wrong temperatures.
What you can check: Not a DIY check. Diagnosed by a technician.

Control board or thermal fuse Pro

A failed control board or a tripped thermal fuse can cut power to the heat circuit.
What you can check: Not DIY. A technician confirms and replaces.

Wrong mode or lock Easy

Delay-start, Sabbath mode, or a self-clean lock can make an oven seem dead.
What you can check: Confirm the oven isn't in a delay, lock, or self-clean cycle before assuming a failure.

Is it worth repairing?

A bake element or a gas igniter is an affordable part and a quick repair — an oven that won't heat is well worth fixing in nearly every case. Replacement only makes sense on a very old range with multiple failing systems.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my Whirlpool oven not heating?

Whirlpool (and Maytag / KitchenAid) ovens that won't heat are usually the simplest fix in the business on electric models — a burned-out bake element you can often see. On gas models it's the igniter. Rule out the control lock and settings first.

Is there a known issue with Whirlpool ovens not heating?

A Whirlpool electric oven that won't heat is a burned-out bake element more often than anything else — and you can usually see the damage. It's one of the cheapest, most satisfying oven repairs there is.

Why won't my electric oven heat up?

On an electric oven the usual culprit is a failed bake element — often with a visible blister or break. A bad temperature sensor or control board is possible but less common. The bake element is an affordable, common repair.

My gas oven igniter glows but there's no flame — why?

That's the classic sign of a weak igniter. As it ages it still glows but can no longer get hot enough to open the gas valve, so the oven never heats. The igniter needs replacing — and because it's gas, it should be done by a technician.

Is an oven that won't heat worth repairing?

Usually yes. The two most common causes — an electric bake element or a gas igniter — are inexpensive parts with quick labor.
