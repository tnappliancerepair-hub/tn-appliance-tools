My dishwasher isn't drying the dishes — what do I do?

Dishes that come out wet at the end of the cycle usually come down to how the dishwasher dries — most modern ones rely on rinse aid and a heat/vent step, and several causes are settings or habits you can fix for free. Here's the order a tech runs through them, cheapest first.

⚠ Safety first: Low-risk checks. Any heating-element or vent repair is done by a tech with the power off.

The most likely causes

No rinse aid Easy DIY check

The most common cause on modern dishwashers. Rinse aid makes water sheet off the dishes so they dry — without it (especially on plastics), dishes stay wet even with a good cycle.

Check it: Fill the rinse-aid dispenser and set it to a higher level. This alone fixes most "not drying" complaints, particularly on newer/efficient models.

Air-dry / eco setting or no heated dry Easy DIY check

If a "heated dry" option is off, or an eco/air-dry cycle is selected, the dishwasher skips the drying heat and leaves dishes damp.

Check it: Turn on Heated Dry (or the "extra dry"/"sanitize" option) and avoid pure eco/air-dry when you want dry dishes.

Loading + plastics + not letting it vent Easy DIY check

Cupped items hold water, overcrowding blocks airflow, and plastics don't hold heat so they stay wet. Opening the door right at the end traps steam too.

Check it: Angle cups/bowls so water runs off, don't overload, put plastics on top, and crack the door open for a few minutes at the end to let steam escape.

Failed heating element or vent/fan Technician

On dishwashers that dry with a heating element or a vent fan, a failed element or stuck vent leaves everything wet even with rinse aid and heated-dry on.

Check it: If rinse aid is full, heated dry is on, and loading is good but dishes are still soaked, a tech checks the heating element or vent/fan.

Is it worth fixing?

Usually a free fix — rinse aid, a setting, or loading habits solve most cases. A failed heating element or vent is an affordable part, far below a new dishwasher.

Common questions

Why is my dishwasher not drying dishes?

Most often it's out of rinse aid, or "heated dry" is turned off. Fill the rinse-aid dispenser, turn on heated dry, and load so cups don't hold water — that fixes the large majority of wet-dish complaints.

Do dishwashers need rinse aid to dry?

Modern ones basically do — rinse aid makes water sheet off so dishes dry, especially plastics. Running without it is the #1 reason dishes come out wet.

How much does it cost to fix a dishwasher that won't dry?

Often nothing (rinse aid or a setting) or an affordable heating element/vent if a part failed. Your $50 Quick Check credits toward the repair.
