# Amazon Store Strategy — becoming the trusted appliance-parts brand

**Companion to** `docs/amazon-dropship-store-plan-2026-07-18.md` (the drop-ship store on our
own site). This doc is the bigger, longer-horizon play: **an owned Amazon brand.** Where the
drop-ship plan monetizes our *website traffic*, this plan builds a **trusted seller brand on
Amazon itself** — the durable channel Teddy wants to maximize.

**Status:** STRATEGY / not started. Real business to build (inventory, Seller Central, ops) —
not passive. But it runs on the exact moat we already have.

---

## The thesis (Teddy, 2026-07-18)
Amazon's parts marketplace **isn't going away** — so maximize it. But we do NOT win it as a
commodity parts seller (a race to the bottom against Encompass, PartSelect, and the OEMs
themselves — we lose on price). We win it as **"the appliance-parts store run by real techs
that tells you the exact part AND how to fix it."** No anonymous parts-flipper on Amazon can
say that. We can. Same moat — expertise + transparency + AI + real reviews — expressed on a
new surface.

## Two kinds of "trust" (don't conflate them)
1. **Trust in Amazon** — why the buy button converts better than a no-name site (Prime, easy
   returns). That's Amazon's, and we borrow it by selling there.
2. **Trust in US as the seller** — the real goal: being the store people *choose* among ten
   sellers of the same part. That's what this doc builds.

---

## OUR UNFAIR ADVANTAGES (why we can become trusted where flippers can't)
1. **Real expertise / E-E-A-T.** Working repair pros since 2012, 1,081 reviews, CSIA-certified.
   Goes straight into the brand story + listings: *"Parts picked by working appliance techs,
   not a warehouse."* Amazon parts sellers are anonymous; we're not.
2. **The AI + the diagnosis — the killer edge.** Nobody selling a heating element on Amazon can
   ALSO hand the buyer the exact part for their model + the how-to + free "is this right?"
   confirmation. **QR code in every box → scan for the fix guide / talk to Ant.** Does three
   things at once: builds trust, kills wrong-part returns, pulls them into our ecosystem.
3. **Fitment data.** Every appliance-parts buyer's #1 fear = "will it fit MY machine?" Nail
   fitment ("fits these 400 models") + free AI confirmation → **return rate drops** → and on
   Amazon low returns protect *ranking*, not just margin (Amazon buries high-return sellers).
   Ant Brain's model→part corpus is the raw material.
4. **Our own external traffic engine.** The advantage most sellers would kill for. They fight
   over Amazon's internal search; WE have 449 pages getting impressions + an email/SMS base +
   a brand. **We can drive external traffic to our Amazon listings — which Amazon rewards with
   higher organic rank.** SEO pages → Amazon store is a flywheel a random seller can't build.
5. **Brand in the home.** Every box ships with an insert — "scan to review · book us · 24/7
   AI." Review velocity + repeat buyers. Reviews are the #1 currency on Amazon.

## The Amazon trust/rank levers (how we express the above)
- **Brand Registry** (needs a registered trademark) → unlocks a **branded storefront**, **A+
  rich content**, Sponsored Brand ads, hijacker protection, and Vine. A registered brand
  *looks* trusted and ranks better.
- **FBA** → Prime badge + Buy Box eligibility + Amazon handles fulfillment AND returns (returns
  resell — the risk-reducer from the drop-ship plan).
- **Review flywheel** → Vine (early reviews) + insert cards + our existing customer base to
  jump-start from zero.
- **Listing quality** → best-in-class titles/images/bullets, **A+ content leading with the
  techs + credentials**, and exhaustive **fitment** (the trust + returns lever).
- **Account health** → low order-defect / low returns / on-time (FBA protects most of this).
- **Win the Buy Box** = FBA (Prime) + competitive price + strong seller metrics.

## The flywheel (how it all compounds)
SEO/`/fix` pages + brand → **external traffic to Amazon listings** → Amazon rewards with organic
rank → sales → **insert cards + Vine drive reviews** → higher rank + Buy Box → more sales →
every box has a QR to Ant → **ecosystem pull + fewer returns + more repeat buyers** → and every
sale + "did it fix it?" trains the model→part corpus → better fitment → fewer returns → higher
rank. The thing that makes money deepens the moat.

## Honest challenges / risks (asked to be real)
- **Competitive + margin-compressed** (~15% referral + FBA fees). Win on niche + trust +
  fitment, not price.
- **Reviews start at zero** — the first few hundred are a grind. Existing base + Vine shorten
  it but it's real work.
- **Returns hurt Amazon RANK**, not just margin — so fitment accuracy is double-important here.
- **Platform risk** — Amazon owns the customer, can change rules, can private-label a hot item.
  → Amazon is A channel, never the ONLY one. Keep our own site + brand as the owned asset.
- **Operational lift** — inventory + capital (FBA = buy stock up front), Seller Central, and
  **sales-tax nexus** in Amazon's warehouse states. Real, doable, proven by thousands.
- **Capital + dead-stock** — only FBA-stock the predictable fat-head SKUs; drop-ship the long
  tail (see the two-channel model in the drop-ship plan).

## Go-to-market: narrow beachhead, then expand
**Do NOT list 10,000 parts.** Win one category deep, then widen.
- **Beachhead = dryer + vent parts + kits** — the category we already dominate (SEO, C-DET,
  the ads focus). Universal/high-volume SKUs = ideal for FBA + clean returns-resell.
- A handful of **hero SKUs done PERFECTLY**: real fitment lists, tech-authored install guides,
  A+ content, the QR insert.
- Point our **external traffic** (dryer-vent SEO pages, email/SMS base) at those listings.
- Grind reviews (Vine + insert + base) → win Buy Box → **then** expand to adjacent categories
  (washer, fridge, range parts).

## Phased rollout
- **Phase 0 — Foundation:** register/confirm the **trademark** (e.g. "TN Appliance") →
  **Amazon Brand Registry** → **Seller Central** account → decide FBA vs FBM. (Trademark can
  take months — start it early, like the API approvals.)
- **Phase 1 — Beachhead:** 3–5 dryer/vent hero SKUs, perfect listings + fitment + A+ + QR
  insert. FBA the universal ones.
- **Phase 2 — Flywheel:** Vine + insert-card reviews; drive external SEO/email traffic to the
  listings; wire the QR → Ant "confirm your part / fix guide" experience.
- **Phase 3 — Expand:** widen the catalog category by category once the beachhead earns reviews
  + Buy Box; consider the branded TN Appliance vent kit (from the drop-ship plan Phase 3).

## How this fits the two-channel store (one coherent system)
- **Amazon FBA = the fat head** (universal, high-volume parts + kits) — this doc.
- **Own-site drop-ship = the long tail** (model-specific parts) — the drop-ship plan.
- **Two buttons map to two paths:** OEM = Marcone drop-ship; Amazon-equivalent = our FBA
  listing. The website and the Amazon store feed each other; neither cannibalizes.

## What we already have vs. what's net-new
- **Have:** the moat (techs + reviews + AI), Ant Brain model→part corpus, `/fix` authority
  pages, 449 impression-getting pages, email/SMS base, brand.
- **Net-new:** trademark + Brand Registry, Seller Central + FBA ops, inventory/capital, the QR
  insert + the "scan → Ant confirm your part" experience, A+ content + listing production.

## Metrics to watch
Buy Box win % · review velocity + avg rating per SKU · **return rate** (the rank-killer) ·
organic rank on target keywords · % of sales from external (our) traffic · category rank ·
repeat-purchase rate · QR-scan → Ant engagement.
