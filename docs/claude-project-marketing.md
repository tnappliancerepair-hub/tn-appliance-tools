## The one-paragraph version

TN Appliance Exchange is a family-owned appliance repair shop, Antioch TN, est. 2012, serving
Middle Tennessee and southeast Louisiana. **4.5 stars across 1,100 Google reviews.** Licensed,
insured, Google Guaranteed. Roughly 95% of the work is home-warranty dispatch — which arrives on
its own. **Marketing exists to grow the other 5%: cash / self-pay customers.** That's the only
work where the shop sets its own price, and it's the growth direction.

---

## The differentiators — everything true, everything checkable

These are the claims marketing leans on. Every one has to survive a skeptical homeowner
spending ten seconds verifying it.

1. **We answer 24/7, including 2am.** An AI receptionist named Ant picks up every call, any
   hour, knows who's calling, and can book the day. No voicemail, no answering service reading
   from a card. This is the single strongest differentiator against every other shop in the market.
2. **We text you right back.** Not "we'll call you within 24 hours."
3. **Real technicians, by name and face.** Five of them, shown on the site with their service
   areas. Family-owned, several of them literally family.
4. **Honest options instead of a sales pitch.** On a cash job the customer sees four prices side
   by side — OEM or aftermarket, you install or we install — with no recommendation attached.
   Most shops in the trade shame customers who price-shop. We don't.
5. **We'll tell you when it isn't worth fixing.** Stated plainly, and meant.
6. **Family shop, since 2012.** Not a franchise, not private equity.

### The naming story

The company's AI assistant is named **Ant**, after the owner's son **Anthony**. There's a
community giving program built on that name — **Anthony's Gift** — $25 off for anyone referred
through a church, food bank, senior center, shelter, or community org. It carries real weight
for the owner; it is not a marketing device. Handle it with care and never make it cute.

## Voice

**Write like a good technician talks to a neighbor.** Plain, warm, specific, a little blunt.

- Lead with the useful fact, not the adjective.
- Short sentences. Say the number.
- Confident, never boastful. Never desperate.
- Humor is fine; hype is not.
- It is always "we," never "TN Appliance Exchange LLC is pleased to..."

**Never:**
- Round a number up. The site once said "1,100+ reviews" when the real count was exactly 1,100 —
  that got corrected on purpose. A claim that inflates is the one thing a skeptic can catch us
  on, and it costs more than the extra digit is worth.
- Publish a part number in customer-facing copy. It invites side-shopping.
- Promise a specific arrival time. The shop runs routed days: "you're one of Jimmy's stops
  Thursday, we text a live window that morning."
- Imply the AI does the diagnosing. **Humans diagnose.** Ant answers phones, texts, schedules,
  and organizes. A real tech decides what's wrong.
- Shame the customer for shopping around, for a cheap part, or for trying it themselves.

## The numbers that matter (and the ones that lie)

- **Local search is won in the map pack, not in blue links.** The site ranks around #5-7
  organically for "appliance repair nashville" and gets almost zero clicks from it, because the
  three-pack sits above organic. **Review velocity is the lever that moves the map pack** — more
  reviews, more often, mentioning the city and the technician by name.
- **"Conversions" in Google Ads currently means a phone rang.** Not a booked job, not a paid
  invoice. Any claim about ad performance built on that number is built on sand. Cost-per-*job*
  is the number that decides where money goes, and it is still being wired up.
- **Segment a trailing average before acting on it.** A 30-day number hides any change made
  inside those 30 days — and hides it in exactly the direction that makes you "fix" something
  already fixed.
- **Warranty volume is not marketing's scoreboard.** It arrives regardless. Judge marketing only
  on cash jobs.

## Open marketing decisions

Worth thinking through here rather than acting on impulse.

1. **Where does the next ad dollar go?** LSA is ~$17/lead and nowhere near its ceiling; Search is
   ~$105 per ringing phone and at capacity. The instinct is "shift to LSA" — but LSA can only be
   *capped*, not pushed. It spends when leads arrive. The real LSA levers are review velocity,
   service-area and job-type coverage, and response speed.
2. **Turn Louisiana back on?** The campaign is built and paused. It covers the North Shore and
   Baton Rouge but has **no ad group for the New Orleans / South Shore side at all** — so one
   technician's entire territory currently has zero paid coverage.
3. **The "1,000+ reviews" claim on ~1,170 pages.** It understates by a hundred but never goes
   stale. A pinned "1,100" is more impressive today and wrong the day review 1,101 lands. The
   durable answer is pulling the live count, which the homepage already does.
4. **Review copy.** Requests go out but rarely ask the customer to name their city or their
   technician — the two things that actually move local ranking.
5. **Social is a real asset being underused.** The owner is a working technician who can film a
   repair in ninety seconds. That's content nobody else in the market can fake.
