00

The thesis

Appliance repair is a trust-poor trade that survives on hiding information — model numbers, part markups, whether a fix is even worth it. Every incentive points at opacity. Ant does the opposite on purpose, and wins because of it.

🎯 North star

The model number isn't the asset — the trust is. We automate the whole job, hand the customer the honest options (fix it, ship you the part, or walk away), and let the outcomes compound into an intelligence no one else has. Transparency isn't a soft value here — it's the moat and the growth engine, the same move.

The plan below is the operating version of that: what's real today, what makes us hard to copy, the three layers in the order we build them, how each one makes money, and the honest risks. It's written for us — so it says what's actually done and what isn't.

02

Why we're hard to copy

A competitor with a weekend and an LLM can clone a screen. They can't clone these four.

1 · Outcomes data — the compounding asset

Every completed job grades Ant Brain's guess against what actually fixed the machine. Accuracy climbs with every TDR. Anyone can bolt AI onto a form; nobody else has the loop of real repairs → real fixes → self-scoring. This is the flywheel, and it only spins for whoever owns the job data. Layer 2 makes it spin faster (more shops = more outcomes).

2 · The founder is a tech

Teddy runs the trucks, knows the vendors, and has felt every pain the product removes. This isn't a founder who read about the trade — it's one the industry trusts. Credibility that can't be hired.

3 · Vendor relationships, years deep

Live integrations and real relationships with AHS/Frontdoor, SquareTrade/ServicePower, NSA, and Marcone/mSupply. A new entrant starts these clocks from zero — some are BD-gated and take months.

4 · Transparency is structurally hard to copy

Our whole edge is handing the customer honest options and shipping them the part. Incumbents survive on markup opacity — they can't match this without gutting their own margins. The decent thing and the strategic thing are the same move.

03

The three layers

Same backend, same brain — three front doors, built in order. Each layer funds and feeds the next.

L1
TN Appliance Exchange — the proving groundOur own shop, fully on Ant. Live today.

Warranty has been ~95% of the work for years; we keep doing that great. The growth direction is self-pay, where we control the whole experience and can make it better than warranty. Everything here is real production traffic — the shop is the lab.

  • Kill the HCP dependency — schedule natively in Ant, retire the last read-only crutch.
  • Self-pay to warranty-parity by EOY — automation makes a cash job as smooth as a warranty one.
  • Feed Ant Brain — auto-predict on every new job so the outcomes loop fills fast.
  • Systematize the office — Teddy covering this week is the prompt; the goal is no single point of human failure.
Done looks like
Any job flows intake → pre-diagnosis → parts staged → tech guided → warranty filed, with a human only where judgment is needed. The three north-star metrics move: first-visit-fix up, calls-per-100-jobs down, days-to-normal down.
L2
Ant for Shops — the SaaSLicense the whole ops brain to other repair shops. Multi-tenant scaffolded (company_id).

The product is the entire system TN runs on: intake, scheduling, the job board, tech tools, warranty automation, Ant Brain. The buyer is the ~10,000 independent appliance-repair shops drowning in the same paperwork we automated away. They become the supply side of Layer 3.

  • Buyer — independent shops (1–20 techs) who live in HCP/spreadsheets/MeisterTask today.
  • Model — free trial to prove value, then per-tech / month once it's earning for them.
  • The network effect — every shop's jobs + TDRs + parts data improve Ant Brain for all shops. The data moat gets deeper with each tenant.
  • Wedge — lead with the one thing that hurts most (warranty submission or parts prediction), then expand into the full ops brain.
Done looks like
A second shop runs a full week on Ant without us hand-holding, their data flows into the shared brain, and the per-tech billing clears. That's the SaaS proven.
L3
The consumer platform — the big ideaHomeowner-facing. The origin story becomes the launchpad.

A homeowner with a broken appliance opens Ant, shows the symptom + model, and gets one of three honest answers — the same transparency that wins in the shop, at national scale.

  • DIY — "$15 part, 20-minute fix, here's the part and the safe how-to. Want us to ship it?"
  • DIY-with-risk — "doable, but 4 hours and some risk. Here's what to expect, or a vetted pro at $X–$Y."
  • Pro-only — "gas / 240V / sealed system / under warranty — don't touch it. Here are two 4.8★ pros near you, open this week."

The TAM flips the whole company. ~10k shops (Layer 2) is a good business; ~120M households with an appliance issue every year is a different one. The pros come from Layer 2. The parts come from the same suppliers. The trust comes from the brand we're building now.

Guardrails (non-negotiable)
Hard auto-gates on dangerous categories (gas, 240V, refrigerant, sealed systems) — no DIY path, ever. "Educational, not professional advice" TOS. Product + liability insurance. Parts under the distributor's warranty (we're the marketplace, not the manufacturer). Pros are 1099, carry their own insurance, verified at intake.

07

The honest risks

What actually threatens this, and the move on each.

Key-person — the office

Too much of the office runs in one person's head (warranty submission especially). Danielle's out this week is the forcing function, not the crisis. Move: Teddy covers + captures how it works (checklist + notes), then automation carries the repetitive load so the desk never depends on one person.

Platform dependency

We ride HCP (leaving), Vapi, Telnyx, Xano, Netlify. Move: own the data — nightly off-site backups, the mined 8-yr history, and killing the HCP crutch. The brain lives on data we hold, not a vendor's.

Consumer liability (Layer 3)

Telling a homeowner they can DIY. Move: hard gates on dangerous categories, educational-not-advice TOS, insurance, marketplace/1099 structure. Designed in from day one, not bolted on.

Competitors converging

Others chasing SaaS-for-repair. Move: validation, not threat — the race is distribution + outcomes data + founder credibility, none of which clone in a weekend. Reserve the ground now (domains, provisional patent) and keep shipping.

Cost / reliability at scale

The automation loop melting the backend, or metered-API surprises. Move: already learned — local queue, SMS circuit-breaker, billing caps, load-shedding. Keep hardening before we add tenants.
