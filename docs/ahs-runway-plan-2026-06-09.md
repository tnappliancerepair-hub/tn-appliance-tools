## The reframe — not a vendor pitch

This is NOT "a software vendor wants 30 minutes." It's **a trusted top provider
bringing AHS a solution to a problem AHS already knows it has.** Pitch on AHS's
pain, in AHS's language:

- AHS's #1 headache = **contractor responsiveness + quality** (slow/unreachable

  schedules, updates the customer, submits a clean claim. Worth far more to AHS
  than a software license.
- To a shop, Ant replaces a dispatcher. **To AHS, Ant upgrades the entire
  network's quality overnight.** Price it against their churn + cost, not CRM
  seats.

## Backward plan from November

**November = reveal with receipts + a pilot proposal.** Everything between now
and then serves that. Three priorities, starting now:

### 1. Bank the operating data — ASAP
Flipping the scheduler + getting the office responsive **this week** isn't just
fire-fighting — it **starts the clock on the metrics shown in November.** Every
week Ant runs clean is a week of ammo. Sooner live = fatter story by fall. The
ops grind IS the path to the masses.

### 2. Instrument the KPIs AHS cares about
Make Ant track the numbers that make an AHS exec say yes (and get promoted for):
- **Response time** (intake → first contact / scheduled)
- **First-visit-fix rate** ← north star; per CLAUDE.md not tracked consistently
  yet — FIX THIS FIRST. Both good ops AND the pitch deck building itself.
- **Claim cycle time** (job done → clean warranty submission)
- **CSAT / customer ratings**
- **Callback / repeat-visit rate**
By November these charts make the case automatically.

### 3. Use the interim video meetings to LISTEN + SEED — not pitch
- **Listen hard** for AHS's stated priorities, exact language, current
  responsiveness/quality initiatives, what they measure contractors on. That
  tells you precisely what to aim the November pitch at.
- **Drop quiet breadcrumbs** — "been building tools internally to stay this
  responsive, getting great results." Plant curiosity, not the blueprint.
- By November they're leaning in — you're closing, not opening.

## Guardrails

1. **IP protection BEFORE any reveal.** The provisional patent (dual-tier +
   confidence model) is cheap insurance — lock it in this window. The danger
   the moment AHS sees the value: "can we just build/own this ourselves?" Show
   the *what* + the *outcomes*; protect the *how*.
2. **Don't tip the other two Pro Council appliance companies.** Peers, but also
   competitors. Your edge is being the *one* who walks in with this in November.
3. **Don't let AHS become your only leg.** They'd be your biggest customer
   (dispatch revenue you depend on) AND your channel — huge concentration. You
   have leverage (Council seat, ratings); so do they (dispatch volume). Keep
   **The Plug + direct-to-shop SaaS** as independent legs so AHS *supercharges*
   you but never *owns* you.
