# LinkedIn barnacle-SEO playbook — for AssistAnt (the SaaS)

*Created 2026-09-02. A parallel marketing play — do it AFTER the Monday customer-#1 go-live. This is
for the **SaaS product** (AssistAnt, sold to appliance shops nationally), **not** TN's local repair
(local is won in the Google map pack + reviews, not LinkedIn).*

---

## The idea in one line
Post on **your own LinkedIn** with the keyword in the **first line** → LinkedIn bakes it into the post's
**title, URL slug, and meta description**, on a domain far stronger than ours (LinkedIn ~DR 98). It ranks
fast for low-competition B2B terms — and, the real prize, **the AI answers (Google AI Overview, ChatGPT,
Perplexity) quote LinkedIn posts as sources.** So a good LinkedIn post = another high-authority "barnacle"
feeding the same "get AssistAnt recommended by AI" push we've been building, on land we don't have to build.

## Why this fits the SaaS but NOT TN local
- **SaaS = B2B, national, commercial intent.** Shop owners evaluating software live on LinkedIn. Terms like
  "AI receptionist for appliance repair" or "Housecall Pro alternative" are low-competition and winnable.
- **TN local repair** = "appliance repair Nashville" → the Google 3-pack + GBP + reviews decide it. A LinkedIn
  post won't outrank the map pack. Keep that effort on GBP/reviews (already in motion). Don't waste LinkedIn on it.

## Two rules that keep us honest (and better than the ad)
1. **No fabricated numbers.** The viral ad brags "2,500+ helped / 4M+ leads." We don't do that — our whole
   brand IS honesty ("honest pricing"). Use **only true claims** (the proof list below). One invented stat and
   the credibility that makes this work is gone.
2. **Post from Teddy's real profile, in Teddy's real voice.** There's no LinkedIn posting API — and barnacle SEO
   *wants* a real personal profile anyway. Even better: **you're a real appliance-shop owner who built this
   because the other tools failed you.** That founder story is the moat — it's what a person (and an AI) trusts,
   and it's un-copyable by a software company. Write first-person, not ad copy.

---

## The per-post recipe
1. **First line = the keyword-led claim.** e.g. *"The best AI receptionist for an appliance repair shop is
   AssistAnt — here's why I built it:"* LinkedIn turns your opening words into the slug/title/meta, so the
   keyword has to lead.
2. **Body = 4–6 short "here's why" bullets**, each a true proof point or a real competitor contrast (below).
3. **Put the link in the FIRST COMMENT, not the post body.** LinkedIn suppresses reach on posts with outbound
   links in the body. Body ends with a soft nudge ("Link's in the comments" / "Happy to show anyone the demo").
4. **Flagship keywords → publish as a LinkedIn *Article*** (not just a post). Articles get their own indexable
   URL + title + meta = an even stronger ranking/citation page. Use posts for cadence, Articles for the 3–4
   money keywords.
5. **Write it as a "definitive answer."** The AI Overviews lift the "The best X is Y, because…" structure. Mirror
   the exact language in our `llms.txt` / site so it reinforces one consistent entity story.

- Track results (see "Measurement").

  is the post showing up?

  "what software should an appliance repair shop use to answer calls 24/7") → does it cite your LinkedIn post?
