## ⚠️ Convergence is real and here

**Ariel Taylor** — *"I built custom software for my husband and am about to
release it to other small appliance repair business owners. Beta testers get a
significant lifetime discount. DM me."* Multiple takers in-thread (Wilson Perez,
Ernest Faber). **This is the exact build-for-spouse → release-to-industry
playbook, recruiting in the same group, right now** — the convergence threat
flagged in CLAUDE.md, now concrete.

**BUT — read what she (and all of them) built: a CRM.** Better software that a
human still has to sit and operate. Nobody in this thread is selling the thing
that removes the human.

## 💡 The positioning this hands us — compete on the LABOR line, not the software line

The whole thread argues over $35 vs $100 vs $230/mo for **software** — while
**Nicholas Molter** (Top contributor) drops the real number: *"2-4 techs, $150/mo
[software] plus $20/hr, 40hr/week for an office receptionist/dispatcher."*

That dispatcher is **~$3,200/month.** The software is the cheap part. **The human
is the real bill** — and half the thread is paying it (the $20/hr receptionist,
Cody's fiancé, Garry's son).

Every competitor (ApplianceOps, Jobber, mhelpdesk, Ariel's build) competes on the
**software line** — who's $X cheaper. **Ant competes on the LABOR line.**

**The headline pitch:**
> *"You're paying ~$150 for software AND ~$3,200 for someone to run it. What if
> you needed neither? Ant answers the calls, schedules the jobs, and chases the
> warranty itself."*

That's not a cheaper CRM. That's **firing the dispatcher.** Different category,
no one else in it. (Belongs in the Ant pitch / About / partner opener.)

## The open lane vs. the crowded one

- **Crowded (don't race here):** "a better/cheaper CRM." ApplianceOps, Jobber,
  Ariel, the homegrown builds. Software a human operates.
- **Open (Ant + The Plug live here):** AI that *replaces the seat* + the data
  flywheel (every tech's fixes making the system smarter for all). Almost nobody
  is building this. Don't build a better Jobber — build the thing that needs no
  dispatcher.

## The un-copyable edge: the operator living the pain

Teddy isn't a software founder guessing at the pain. He's the operator bleeding
from it in real time — a drowning office, a dispatcher dependence he resents,
warranty companies hammering the phones. **That's the product demo and the
credibility.** Ariel sells software her husband uses; Teddy *is* the customer,
building the cure from inside the fire. That story can't be cloned.
