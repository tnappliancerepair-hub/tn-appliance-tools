Common Causes

What Usually Goes Wrong

Drain pump or drain path

A failed drain pump, a clogged hose, a stuck check valve, or debris and broken glass in the sump leave standing water at the bottom. The most common dishwasher complaint we see.

Wash pump & spray arms

If dishes come out gritty, the wash pump may be worn or the spray arms clogged with hard-water scale and food. Common in Middle TN and Louisiana's harder water.

Water inlet valve

A failing inlet valve under-fills the tub, so there's never enough water to clean — or it sticks open and overfills and leaks.

Door latch & switch

If the cycle won't start, the door latch or its switch often isn't telling the control the door is shut. An inexpensive, high-frequency fix.

Heating element & control board

No-heat washes and won't-dry complaints trace to the heating element or thermostat; dead panels and stuck cycles point to the control board.

Is It Worth Fixing?

Our Philosophy on Repair vs. Replace

For dishwashers, the math usually favors repair. Pumps, valves, latches, and spray arms are affordable and readily available, and the labor is straightforward on most platforms. The harder call is a failed control board on a newer unit, or a tub that's cracked or rusted through. The replacement calculator gives you a quick framing, and our technicians give you the real numbers on your specific machine — every situation is different.

FAQ

People Also Ask

How much does dishwasher repair cost?
Most dishwasher repairs land in the $150–$350 range. Common fixes like a drain pump, door latch, or water inlet valve are on the lower end; control boards and motors run higher. Your $50 Quick Check or $100 in-home diagnostic credits directly toward the repair.
Why is my dishwasher not draining?
The most common causes are a clogged or failed drain pump, a blocked drain hose or air gap, or a stuck check valve. Food debris and broken glass in the sump are frequent culprits. A technician confirms the exact cause before any part is ordered.
Why isn't my dishwasher cleaning the dishes?
Usually a clogged spray arm, a worn wash pump, a failed water inlet valve restricting fill, or a heating element that isn't bringing the water up to temperature. We diagnose which one it is rather than guessing.
Is it worth repairing a dishwasher?
Often yes — pumps, valves, latches, and spray arms are affordable, available parts. The harder call is a failed control board on a newer unit. Our technicians give you honest repair-vs-replace numbers for your specific model.
